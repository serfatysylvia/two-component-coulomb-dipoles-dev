\begin{abstract}
  We consider the two-dimensional two-component plasma, or Coulomb gas, consisting of $N$ positive and $N$ negative charges with logarithmic interaction. We introduce a suitable regularization of the interaction by smearing the charges over a small length scale $\lambda$, which allows us to give meaning to the system in the continuum at any temperature. We provide an expansion of the free energy \edblue{for each inverse temperature $\beta\ge2$}, as $N \to \infty$ and $\lambda \to 0$. Doing so allows us to show that, for $\beta \geq 2$, the charges (for the most part) pair into neutral dipoles of very small size as $\lambda \to 0$. This complements the prior work of Leblé, Zeitouni, and the second author, which proved that this does not happen for $\beta < 2$, thereby implying a transition at $\beta = 2$ from free charges to dipole pairs. Moreover, we obtain an estimate on the size of linear statistics.

  The description in terms of dipoles is made via a decomposition into nearest-neighbor graphs of the point configurations, \`a la Gunson-Panta. This is combined with new energy estimates obtained via an electric reformulation of the interaction energy and a ball-growth method, which are expressed in terms of the nearest-neighbor graph distances only. In this way, the model is compared to a reduced nearest-neighbor interaction model, by showing the relative smallness of the dipole-dipole interactions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Setting of the problem}
In the 1970s, Kosterlitz and Thouless \cite{Kosterlitz1974,Kosterlitz1973} and independently Berezinsky \cite{Berezinsky1970fr} predicted a completely new type of phase transition without long-range order  in two-dimensional  systems, now called the Berezinsky-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition. This celebrated transition (see \cite{bietenholz2016berezinskii} for a review) was predicted to happen in a whole range of models which exhibit quantized vortices in a neutral ensemble, more specifically the XY or ``rotator" spin model, models of dislocations and superfluids, and it  has important consequences for condensed matter physics.

The transition in the XY model is probably the one that has attracted the most attention in the mathematical physics community.
In this model unit spins are sampled on a lattice, constituting a $\mathbb{U}(1)$ analogue of the Ising model.  The correlation function between distant spins decays exponentially above the BKT transition temperature and as a power law below \cite{mcbryanspencer,frohlichspencer,mkp1,mkp2,bricmont}.

This transition is explained by the formation of topological vortices, which are points around which the spin field has a nonzero degree or winding number. Below the transition temperature, vortices are bound into dipole pairs (i.e.,~pairs of vortices with opposite winding numbers), while above the transition temperature, vortices are like ``free particles".

In the original papers, as well as in subsequent research, it is expected that in the XY model (or its simplified variant, the Villain model) the energy of the system can be split into a vortex-gas energy and a spin-wave contribution, corresponding to the fluctuations around the vortex configurations \cite{Kosterlitz1974,Kosterlitz1973,kennedy}.
This statement turns out to be delicate to prove rigorously, and this has attracted the attention of researchers, even recently \cite{garban2020statistical,garban2020quantitative}.

Once the spin-wave contribution can be separated, the model reduces to a (2D) gas of \edblue{positive and negative charges} with logarithmic interaction, which can also be called a two-component plasma, or (two-component) Coulomb gas. The Coulomb gas is thus a fundamental model on which to understand the BKT transition, as  seen in the original paper  of Kosterlitz \cite{Kosterlitz1973}.

The lattice (two-component) Coulomb gas was  studied in the seminal work of Fr\"ohlich-Spencer \cite{frohlichspencer} via the sine-Gordon representation and expansions into multipole ensembles, allowing them to analyze the decay rate of correlation functions, thus giving the first proof of the BKT transition (see also \cite{mcbryanspencer,mkp1,mkp2}).

The Coulomb gas may as well be studied in the continuum rather than on a lattice, and is expected to exhibit the same transition between a situation with free vortices and a situation with vortices of opposite sign bound in dipole pairs. There is a subtlety, however, due to the fact that this ``dipole transition" should happen at inverse temperature $\beta=2$ in the units we use, while the KT transition between exponentially and algebraically decaying correlations is expected to happen at $\beta=4$ in this setting. Also, it is a little delicate to directly compare the situation of the Coulomb gas in the continuum where one takes a fixed number $N$ of charges  of each sign, corresponding to a canonical ensemble, and the situation of the XY model, corresponding to a grand-canonical ensemble where the number of vortices is not prescribed.

Here we will focus on the continuum Coulomb gas or ``two-component plasma" in the canonical case and we  will provide a proof of the ``dipole transition" based simply on the analysis of dipole pairs  via large deviations techniques that allow us to weigh their energy and entropy costs, in some sense very close to the arguments  and computations found in the original papers \cite{Kosterlitz1973,Kosterlitz1974} and also in the seminal paper \cite{GunPan}.

\ed{
It was predicted in   \cite{frohlichspencer,BenfattoGallavottiNicolo,GallavottiNicolo} that such a system also has an infinite  sequence of  transitions at
\begin{equation}\label{defbetap}\beta_p :=  4 - \frac{2}{p},\quad \text{where $p\in \mathbb{N}^*$}, \end{equation}
which corresponds to the divergence (at small scale) of the free energy of  multipoles of size $2p$ (i.e.~sets of $p$ dipoles clustered together).
This was mostly analyzed by sine-Gordon transformation, using field theory techniques and Mayer expansions \cite{frohlichspencer,BenfattoGallavottiNicolo,GallavottiNicolo,dimockhurd}.  Analyzing these multipole transitions, and obtaining refined asymptotic expansions via cluster expansions,  is the object of our subsequent paper \cite{BS2}.}

\subsection{Model}
We first consider the continuum Coulomb gas, defined as an ensemble with  configurations $(\XN, \YN)$ (with $X_N=(x_1, \dots, x_N) \in \Lambda^N $ and $Y_N=(y_1, \dots, y_N)\in \Lambda^N$)  of $N$ positive and $N$ negative particles  (or vortices with degrees $+1$ or $-1$) in the blown-up cube $\Lambda = [0,\sqrt{N}]^2$ of $\R^2$, having  energy
\begin{equation}\label{1.1} \F(\XN, \YN)= \hal \( \sum_{i\neq j} \bigl(- \log |x_i-x_j|-\log |y_i-y_j|\bigr)+ 2 \sum_{i,j} \log |x_i-y_j|\),\end{equation} and
consider the (canonical) ensemble
\begin{equation}\label{ZGP} \frac{1}{Z_{N,\beta}} \exp\(- \beta \F(X_N, Y_N) \) \dd X_N \dd Y_N ,\end{equation}
with $\dd X_N$ and $\dd Y_N$ the \edblue{Lebesgue measures} on $\Lambda^N$.
This model was studied in particular in \cite{GunPan,DeutschLavaud}, and more recently in \cite{LSZ}.
The integral defining $\ZNbeta$ diverges as soon as $\beta \ge 2$, \edblue{since the Boltzmann weight of very short dipoles is not integrable}, which corresponds to the dipole transition.
The ensemble \eqref{ZGP} was thus studied only in the regime $\beta <2$ in the aforementioned works \cite{GunPan,DeutschLavaud,LSZ}.
The latest results of \cite{LSZ}, building on important insights from \cite{GunPan} and techniques developed for the study of the one-component Coulomb gas in \cite{SS2d,RougSer,LS1,LS2}, show an expansion of $\log \ZNbeta$ as $N \to \infty$, as well as a large deviations principle on point processes, which characterize a situation with free interacting particles, with competition between the attraction of opposite charges and the entropic repulsion. This corresponds to temperatures above the critical temperature.

In order to study such a system for $\beta \ge 2$, a truncation of the interaction is needed, as already recognized in \cite{Kosterlitz1974,Kosterlitz1973} and analyzed in \cite{frohlichspencer} (see also the discussion in \edblue{\cite{LRVboundary}}).
For brevity, let us always denote
\begin{equation}
  \g(x)=-\log |x|,
\end{equation}
and we will abuse notation by considering $\g$ as a function on $\R^2$ or on $\R$ depending on the context.

Truncating the interaction involves introducing a small length scale $\lambda$ and {\it renormalizing} the divergent part of the free energy as $\lambda\to 0$. A natural proposed way is to truncate the energy at a distance $\lambda$ and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F2}
  \edblue{\hal \sum_{i,j}\bigl(\g(x_i-x_j)\wedge\g(\lambda)+\g(y_i-y_j)\wedge\g(\lambda)-2\bigl(\g(x_i-y_j)\wedge\g(\lambda)\bigr)\bigr)-N\g(\lambda),}
\end{equation}
where $\wedge $ denotes the minimum of two numbers.

\edblue{We use a variant convenient for our techniques:} instead of truncating $\g$ we consider charges smeared on discs of radius $\lambda$, with $\lambda$ small, interacting otherwise in the normal Coulomb fashion: letting $\delta_{z}^{(\lambda)}$ denote the uniform measure of mass $1$ supported on $ B(z, \lambda)$, we let
\begin{equation} \label{defkappa}
  \kappa:= \iint \g(x-y) \delta_0^{(1)}(x)\delta_0^{(1)}(y),\end{equation}
and observe, by scaling, that
\begin{equation}\label{geta0}\iint \g(x-y) \delta_0^{(\lambda)} (x) \delta_0^{(\lambda)}(y)= \g(\lambda)+ \kappa.\end{equation}

We then consider the energy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F3}
  \F_\lambda(\XN, \YN)= \hal \iint \g(x-y) \dd\( \sum_{i=1}^N \bigl(\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)}-\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)}\bigr)\)(x)
  \dd\( \sum_{i=1}^N \bigl(\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)}-\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)}\bigr)\)(y) -  N   ( \g( \lambda)+\kappa).
\end{equation}
Here, compared to \eqref{1.1} we have reinserted the self-interaction terms which are no longer infinite  but equal to $\g(\lambda) + \kappa$,
and then subtracted them off.

We will denote by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:defgeta} \g_\lambda(z)= \iint \g(x-y) \delta_0^{(\lambda)}(x)\delta_z^{(\lambda)} (y),
\end{equation} the effective interaction between two points at distance $|z|$.  Since the convolution  $\g* \delta_0^{(\lambda)}$ is  harmonic outside of $B(0, \lambda)$, it follows from the mean-value theorem (or Newton's theorem) that
\begin{equation}  \g_\lambda(z)=  \int \g* \delta^{(\lambda)}_0 \delta_z^{(\lambda)} = \g(z) \quad \text{for } |z|\ge 2\lambda,\end{equation}  so $\g_\lambda$ coincides with $\g$ at large enough distance.

\edblue{Thus $\F_\lambda$ is obtained from \eqref{eq:F2} by replacing the truncated kernel by $\g_\lambda$, including in the self-interaction term\edgreen{, and subtracting $N\g_\lambda(0)=N(\g(\lambda)+\kappa)$ instead of $N\g(\lambda)$}. If all pairwise distances are at least $2\lambda$, then $\F_\lambda=\F$. In particular, $\F_\lambda\to\F$ as $\lambda\to0$ for configurations of distinct points. The kernel $\g_1$ and the constant $\kappa$ depend on the smearing profile, as does $\mathcal Z_\beta$ below when $\beta>2$.}
Finally, we could in principle use charges smeared on circles instead of discs as in previous works \cite{petrache2017next,LS1,LS2}. It does make the initial computations simpler but the potential generated by a circle is too singular for our needs here.
\smallskip

We  thus work with \eqref{eq:F3} and study
\begin{equation} \label{defP}
  \dd\P= \frac{1}{\Z} \exp\(- \beta \F_\lambda(\XN, \YN)\) \dd X_N \dd Y_N
\end{equation}
in the limit where $\lambda$ tends to zero, where
\begin{equation} \label{eq:ZRV}\Z= \int_{\Lambda^{N}\times \Lambda^{N}} \exp\(-\beta \F_\lambda(\XN, \YN)\)  \dd \XN \dd\YN.
\end{equation}
When $\beta <2$ one can set $\lambda=0$ and recover the model studied in \cite{LSZ}, but when \edblue{$\beta\ge2$} one expects $\log \Z$ to diverge as  $\lambda \to 0$.
The picture that emerges from the literature, mostly based on the sine-Gordon representation,
is well-described in \edblue{\cite{LRVboundary}}:
for $\beta>2$, the divergence of the system as $\lambda\to 0$ corresponds to the pairing of short dipoles (of length scale $\lambda$), and the transition at $\beta=2$ is followed as $\beta$ increases by a sequence of transitions corresponding to the formation of a subdominant proportion of  multipoles (quadrupoles, sextupoles, etc.) as the temperature is decreased and the entropic repulsion becomes less strong \cite{frohlichspencer}. This is due to the weakly attractive nature of the dipole-dipole interaction. When $\beta $ reaches $4$, in the grand canonical setting (when the number of particles is not fixed) the system is expected to collapse under the attraction of the dipoles, as first shown in \cite{Frohlich} via a Euclidean Field Theory approach. However, we will see that it is not the case in the canonical setting  here.

Our main goal here is to analyze  \eqref{eq:F3}--\eqref{defP} via a simple and direct approach based solely on  energy and entropy, precisely via electrostatic estimates for the energy. We obtain below a precise free energy expansion as $N \to \infty$ and $\lambda \to 0$, and use it to prove that configurations mostly form \edblue{isolated neutral dipoles} for all $\beta \in [2,+\infty)$ (this shows that  the multipoles, though present, concern only a vanishing fraction of the particles). Combined with the description of \cite{LSZ}, it constitutes a  proof of the dipole transition, and we hope this point of view will also inform the understanding of the BKT transition. We also address the important question of the fluctuations of the (two-component) Coulomb gas.

Note that the two-component Coulomb gas or plasma is quite different from the one-component Coulomb gas or plasma, which consists only of positively charged particles repelling each other and confined by an external potential, \edblue{or, in the jellium case, by} a uniform negative background charge. The one-component Coulomb gas never diverges nor forms dipole pairs, but rather the particle density converges macroscopically to an equilibrium measure limit (dictated by the external potential) while at the microscopic scale the particles arrange themselves in more and more ordered point patterns as temperature decreases. In fact the system is expected to crystallize at zero temperature, at least in low dimensions. There has been much progress on the one-component plasma in recent years, including free energy expansion \cite{Lieb1997,LS1,Armstrong2019LocalLA,serfaty2020gaussian}, local laws for the distribution of points down to the microscale \cite{leble2017local,Armstrong2019LocalLA}, variational characterization of the limiting point processes \cite{LS1}, and CLTs for the fluctuations of linear statistics \edblue{\cite{ameur2011fluctuations,rider2007noise,bauerschmidt2016two,LS2,serfaty2020gaussian}}.  We refer to \cite{serfaty2024lectures} for a comprehensive description.

The main points in common with  \cite{SS2d,LS1,leble2017local,LS2,Armstrong2019LocalLA,serfaty2020gaussian} but also with \cite{LSZ} will be the general philosophy of electrostatic energy and  large deviations techniques, as well as the {\it electric formulation} of the energy that we present just below.

\subsection{Main results}

The electric formulation mentioned above consists in reexpressing the energy in terms of the {\it electric potential} $h_\lambda$ generated by the configuration $(X_N, Y_N)$ and defined as a function {\it over all} $\R^2$  by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heta}h_\lambda[X_N, Y_N]:= \g* \( \sum_{i=1}^N \bigl(\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)}-\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)}\bigr)\),\end{equation} where $*$ denotes the convolution.
In the sequel, we will most often drop the $[X_N, Y_N]$ dependence in the notation.

Note that by definition of $\g$, $h_\lambda$ satisfies  the Poisson equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Deltaheta}
  -\Delta h_\lambda[X_N, Y_N]= 2\pi \( \sum_{i=1}^N \bigl(\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)}-\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)}\bigr)\).\end{equation}
A direct insertion into \eqref{eq:F3} and integration by parts using \eqref{eq:Deltaheta} yields the following electric rewriting of the energy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rewrF}\F_\lambda(\XN, Y_N)= \frac{1}{4\pi} \int_{\R^2} \left|\nab h_\lambda[X_N, Y_N]\right|^2 -  N (\g(\lambda)+\kappa).\end{equation}
\edblue{Neutrality gives $h_\lambda(x)=O(|x|^{-1})$ and $\nabla h_\lambda(x)=O(|x|^{-2})$ at infinity. A smooth cutoff tending to~$1$ justifies the integration by parts.}

Before stating our main result, let us introduce some more notation.
\edblue{For $\beta>2$,} let us define the probability measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defmu}
  \dd \mu_\beta(r):=\frac{2\pi}{\mathcal Z_\beta}\exp\Bigr(\beta\g_1(r)\Bigr)\indic_{\R^+}(r)r \dd r,
\end{equation}
where $\g_1$ was defined in \eqref{eq:defgeta} and $\mathcal Z_\beta$ \edblue{is given by}
\begin{equation}\label{def:Cbeta}
  \mathcal Z_{\beta}:=\begin{cases}
    2\pi\int_0^{\infty}\exp\Bigr(\beta\g_1(r)\Bigr)r \dd r & \text{if $\beta>2$}\\
    2\pi & \text{if $\beta=2$}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\edblue{For $\beta>2$, we have $0<\mathcal Z_\beta<\infty$, since $\g_1$ is bounded on $[0,2]$ and $e^{\beta\g_1(r)}r=r^{1-\beta}$ for $r\ge2$. This} constant is related to our precise way of smearing the Dirac charges and corresponds to the interaction of overlapping disc charges.

We will denote by $\edblue{(z_1,\dots,z_{2N})=(x_1,\dots,x_N,y_1,\dots,y_N)}$ the collection of all points (positive or negative) and set $d_i=1$ if $i\in [1,N]$ and $d_i=-1$ if $i\in [N+1, 2N]$.
\edblue{We exclude configurations with coinciding points or equal distances between distinct unordered pairs. This is a null set by Fubini. We denote by $\phi_1(i)$ the index of the nearest neighbor to $z_i$.}
A generic configuration can be quite complicated. In particular, it is not obvious how to extract its dipoles.
In this paper, dipoles are defined  via the nearest neighbor graph of a configuration, following important ideas of \cite{GunPan}. This definition ignores, in the first pass, the sign of the particle, by considering only for each point   $z_i$, its first nearest neighbor \edblue{$z_{\phi_1(i)}$} of arbitrary sign, and \edblue{its scaled, truncated nearest neighbor distance $\rr_1(z_i)$ defined in \eqref{nn2}}.
Other definitions are possible, and we will  pursue another approach in forthcoming work.

The nearest-neighbor graph is a (directed) graph on $\{1, \dots, 2N\}$ with an edge from $i$ to $j=\phi_1(i)$, i.e.~if $z_j$ is the nearest neighbor to $z_i$.  It is a general fact (see \edblue{\cite{eppstein1997}}) that the connected components of such nearest-neighbor graphs are all formed of a 2-cycle with trees possibly attached to them. Note that points in a 2-cycle are characterized by the fact that $\phi_1\circ \phi_1(i)=i$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{cycle-pics.pdf}
  \caption{A nearest-neighbor graph with Gunson-Panta variables}
\end{figure}

We will say that a 2-cycle is isolated if it has no tree attached to it, i.e.~forms a whole connected component of the graph. We will say that such a 2-cycle is an isolated neutral 2-cycle if in addition it connects two points of opposite sign, i.e.,~it connects $i$ and $j$ with $d_i d_j=-1$. We also call such 2-cycles ``isolated  dipoles." \edgreen{The length of the dipole $\{i,j\}$ is $|z_i-z_j|$.}

Our first theorem provides a free energy expansion and a concentration on dipole configurations.
Here, and throughout the paper, $C_\beta, c_\beta$ denote positive constants depending only on $\beta$. \edblue{We take $M_0=36$.}

\begin{theo}\label{theorem:th1}
  \edblue{For $\beta\ge2$ and $0<\lambda<e^{-1}$, let}
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:defgammal}
    \omega_\lambda:=\begin{cases}
      \edgreen{|\log \lambda|^{-\frac{1}{5}}} & \text{if $\beta=2$}\\
      \edblue{\lambda^{\frac{2(\beta-2)}{12-\beta+2M_0}}} &\text{if $\beta\in (2,4)$}\\
      \edgreen{(\lambda|\log \lambda|)^{\frac{2}{6+M_0}}} & \text{if $\beta=4$}\\
      \edgreen{\lambda^{\frac{2}{4+M_0}}} & \text{if $\beta>4$},
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation} and observe that $\omega_\lambda = o_\lambda(1)$ as $\lambda \to 0$.
  \edblue{For each $\beta\ge2$, there exists $\lambda_\beta\in(0,e^{-1})$ such that the following bounds hold uniformly for $N\ge2$ and $0<\lambda<\lambda_\beta$.}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \edblue{We have $0<\Z<\infty$ and the free energy expansion}
    \begin{multline}\label{eq:devlogz}
      \log \Z = 2N \log N + N  \Big( (2-\beta) (\log \lambda)\indic_{\beta>2}+(\log|\log\lambda|)\indic_{\beta=2}+ (\log \mathcal Z_{\beta}-1)+  O_\beta(\omega_\lambda)\Big)\\\edblue{+O_\beta(\log(N+1))}. \end{multline}
    \item  The energy essentially reduces to the nearest neighbor interactions of dipoles (or neutral 2-cycles) in the sense that
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:momexp}
      \log \Esp_{\P}\Bigr[ \exp\Bigr(\beta\Bigr(\F_\lambda-\tilde{\F}_\lambda\Bigr)\Bigr) \Bigr]\leq C_\beta N\omega_\lambda\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)},
    \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation*}
      \tilde{\F}_\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)})\indic_{\phi_1\circ\phi_1(i)=i,d_id_{\phi_1(i)}=-1}.
    \end{equation*}
    \item The Gibbs measure is concentrated on configurations made  mostly  of  isolated dipoles\edblue{, with the following bounds on their number and lengths}:\\
    $\bullet$ \edblue{For $p_0\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$,} letting $\mc{A}(p_0)$ be the event on which there are exactly $p_0$ isolated dipoles, we have
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:b1}
      \log \P(\mc{A}(p_0))\leq -c_\beta N \Bigr(1-\frac{p_0}{N}\Bigr)^2+O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)}.
    \end{equation}
    $\bullet$ \edblue{For $p_0'\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$ and $R>0$, let $\mc{B}(R,p_0')$ be the event on which there are exactly $p_0'$ isolated dipoles of length larger than $R$.} \edblue{For $\beta>2$ and $R\ge2\lambda$, let} $x_0:=\frac{p_0'}{N}$ and $\alpha:=(\frac{\lambda}{R})^{\beta-2}$. \edblue{There exist $C_\beta,c_\beta>0$ such that if $x_0\geq C_\beta\alpha$, then}
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:b2}
      \log \P(\mc{B}(R,p_0'))\leq  -\edblue{c_\beta} N x_0\log \Bigr(\frac{x_0}{\alpha}\Bigr)+\edgreen{O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)}\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)}.
    \end{equation}
    \edblue{$\bullet$ For $\beta=2$, there exists $C>0$ such that, for $N\ge2$, $0<\lambda<\lambda_2$, $2\lambda\le R\le1$ and $p_0'\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical lengths}
      \P\bigl(\mc B(R,p_0')\bigr)
      \le (N+1)^C\exp\left[-p_0'+CN\left(\omega_\lambda+
      \edgreen{\frac{\log(1/R)}{|\log\lambda|}}\right)\right].
    \end{equation}}
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theo}
The error rate $\omega_\lambda$ in the present proof is not sharp and could be optimized. However, we do not pursue this goal here. \edblue{A sharper expansion is obtained in \cite{BS2}.}

\medskip

The formula \eqref{eq:devlogz} can be compared with the formula  for $\beta<2$ obtained in \cite{LSZ}\footnote{Note that the scaling used in  \cite{LSZ} is different in the sense that the particles are in a box of size $1$.} and this already exhibits a transition at $\beta=2$, since the divergence in $\lambda$ is present only for $\beta \ge 2$.
\edblue{When the thermodynamic limit exists, we write}
\begin{equation}\label{thermolim}
  f(\beta,\lambda)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\bigl(\log\Z-2N\log N\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $\beta<2$, \eqref{thermolim}  is proven in \cite{LSZ} (with $\lambda=0$) and a variational characterization of $f$ is also provided there:
\begin{equation} f(\beta, 0)=  -\edblue{\inf}_{P\in \mathcal P} \mathcal F_\beta(P),\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P $ corresponds to the space of stationary signed point processes of intensity $1$ (each species has intensity 1), and
$\mathcal F_\beta$ \edblue{is the free-energy functional appearing there. It} is the sum of $\beta$ times a suitable ``renormalized energy" of point processes (an infinite volume Coulomb interaction energy) and a specific relative entropy with respect to \edblue{two independent Poisson point processes of intensity~$1$, one for each sign}. We see that in that regime we have free particles of positive and negative charges, whose positions \edblue{are} governed by the minimization of $\mathcal F_\beta$.

\edblue{Whenever \eqref{thermolim} exists, \eqref{eq:devlogz} gives, for $\beta\ge2$,}
\begin{equation}\label{valeurf}
  f(\beta, \lambda) = (2-\beta) \log \lambda + (\log |\log \lambda|) \indic_{\beta=2}+  \log \mathcal Z_{\beta} -1+ o_\lambda(1).\end{equation}
Here the energy is dominated by pure dipole energy, demonstrating the transition from free particles to bound pairs.
The question of the sharp rate of convergence $o_\lambda(1)$ is very important as it encodes the multipole transitions.

We finally address the important question of the fluctuations of linear statistics. We provide an energetic control, similar in spirit to \cite[Prop. 2.5]{LS2}, \edblue{giving a fluctuation bound at scale $\sqrt N\,\omega_\lambda^{1/4}$ for Lipschitz test-functions. The tail below tends to zero when $\sqrt N\,\omega_\lambda\to\infty$.} One could obtain a better rate of fluctuations in $o(N^{1/2})$ at fixed $\lambda$, but this would require more involved arguments.

\begin{theo}\label{prop:linear}
  \edblue{For $\beta\ge2$, there exist $\lambda_\beta\in(0,e^{-1})$\edgreen{, possibly smaller than in Theorem~\ref{theorem:th1},} and $M_\beta,C_\beta>0$ with the following property.} Consider a compactly supported Lipschitz test-function $\xi_0 :\R^2 \to \R$ and set $\xi=\xi_0(N^{-\nicefrac{1}{2}}\cdot)$. Define  $$\Fluct_N(\xi):= \int_{\Lambda} \xi \, \dd \Big(\sum_{i=1}^N \bigl(\delta_{x_i}-\delta_{y_i}\bigr)\Big).$$
  \edblue{For every $M>M_\beta$, there is $c_{\beta,M}>0$, independent of $\xi_0$, such that for $N\ge2$ and $0<\lambda<\lambda_\beta$,}
  \begin{equation*}
    \P\( \Fluct_N(\xi)^2 \edblue{>} M N \ed{\omega_\lambda^{\nicefrac{1}{2}}}\Vert\nabla\xi_0\Vert_{L^{\infty}}^2 \)\leq C_\beta e^{-c_{\beta,M} N^{\nicefrac{1}{2}}\edblue{\omega_\lambda}},
  \end{equation*}
  where $\omega_\lambda$ is as in  \eqref{eq:defgammal}.
\end{theo}
\edblue{Here $\|\nabla\xi_0\|_{L^\infty}$ is the essential supremum of the a.e. gradient, equal to the Lipschitz constant of $\xi_0$.}

\edblue{\hypersetup{linkcolor=blue}\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{\textcolor{blue}{\arabic{footnote}}}Theorems~\ref{theorem:th1} and~\ref{prop:linear} have been formalized in Lean~4.\footnote{\edblue{Formalization available at : \hypersetup{urlcolor=blue}\url{https://github.com/serfatysylvia/two-component-coulomb-dev}.}}}

\subsection{Method}
In order to prove Theorem \ref{theorem:th1}
we need to obtain sharp upper and lower bounds on the energy of a configuration in terms of its dipoles, and good corresponding ``volume estimates".
This amounts to comparing the original model to a reduced nearest neighbor model
and the main work is to prove that the two models are close. Their closeness then allows us to show that the Gibbs measure concentrates on dipole configurations, control their sizes and control linear statistics.

Combining the description in terms of the nearest neighbor graph \`a la Gunson-Panta with the electric formulation \eqref{eq:rewrF} turns out to be an efficient way of  obtaining energy lower bounds. This is done by a {\it ball-growth method}, which consists in expanding the \edblue{smeared charges} $\delta_{z_i}^{(\lambda)} $ into a charge  $\delta_{z_i}^{(\rr_1(z_i))}$ of the same mass but supported in the disc $ B(z_i, \rr_1(z_i))$. \edblue{Every disc whose radius grows stays disjoint from the other discs, so Newton's theorem preserves the pairwise interactions.} This, however, misses a term of order 1 in the interaction energy of each dipole.
In order to avoid this loss, we push the method further by examining second nearest neighbor distances $\rr_2(z_i)$ \edblue{and grow each pair to a common radius determined by these distances}.
In the works on the one-component plasma \cite{LS2,Armstrong2019LocalLA,serfaty2020gaussian}, see also \cite[Chap. 4]{serfaty2024lectures}, we were able to take advantage of the fact that when all the charges are positive, the interaction of  disc charges decreases when the radii are increased.  This is no longer the case in a situation with different charge signs, and so instead of using monotonicity, we proceed to a direct estimation of the change in the interaction when the discs are increased. We then obtain an estimate which bounds from below the energy {\it in terms of nearest neighbor interactions only}.
This part contains the most delicate estimates as we need to control the contributions of the dipole-dipole interactions and the errors they create, something not handled in \cite{GunPan,LSZ}. We   provide more precise estimates than in those papers  via  new ideas.

An important feature of this dipole decomposition  lower bound is that it is amenable to integration in phase-space with the method of Gunson-Panta, as revisited in \cite{LSZ}, of separating the integral over types of nearest neighbor graphs.
\edblue{For the matching lower bound on the free energy, we bound the energy from above by a sum of local Neumann energies, one for each dipole.}
\edblue{Together with the constrained upper bounds, this yields concentration on dipole configurations, as in \eqref{eq:b1}.}

The bound on fluctuations of Theorem \ref{prop:linear} is obtained by using the electric formulation of the energy which allows us to show that the energy directly controls the fluctuations,  and combining it with  the  ball-growth method which is this time used by inflating the balls to  an intermediate  length scale $\gamma $ much larger than $\lambda$ and much smaller than $1$.

\medskip

{\bf Plan of the paper:}  Section \ref{sec2} is devoted to the proof of the energy lower bound in terms of nearest neighbor interactions  with dipolar interaction errors,  via the ball-growth method. In Section \ref{sec3}, this lower bound is inserted into the Gibbs measure to produce, via a suitable decomposition of the phase-space and large deviation estimates, the free energy upper bound.
Section \ref{sec4} provides a matching lower bound  by explicit construction of configurations and estimates of their free energy. Finally, Section \ref{sec5} provides the proof of Theorem \ref{prop:linear}.

\section{Nearest neighbor lower bound}\label{sec2}
\edblue{Throughout this section, we assume $N\ge2$ and work outside the null set excluded above.}

\subsection{Definitions}
\subsubsection*{Signed point configurations} With the shortcut $Z_{2N}$ for $(X_N, Y_N)$ and $d_i=\pm 1$ for their signs,
we are able to rewrite \eqref{eq:heta} as
\begin{equation*}
  h_\lambda=\g* \( \sum_{i=1}^{2N} d_i \delta_{z_i}^{(\lambda)}\),
\end{equation*}
with
\begin{equation} \label{Deltah} -\Delta h_\lambda= 2\pi \sum_{i=1}^{2N} d_i \delta_{z_i}^{(\lambda)}.\end{equation}

When increasing the discs we will also denote similarly
for any vector $\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_1, \dots , \alpha_{2N})$ in $\edblue{(0,\infty)^{2N}}$
\begin{equation}\label{defhalpha} h_{\vec{\alpha}} = \g* \( \sum_{i=1}^{2N} d_i \delta_{z_i}^{(\alpha_i)} \).\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Successive nearest neighbor distances}
First we set
\begin{equation}\label{nn2}\rr_1(z_i):=  \(\frac14 \min_{j\neq i} |z_i-z_j| \)\vee \lambda,
\end{equation}   then for each $\edblue{2\le p\le2N-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{nn3}  \rr_p(z_i) := \(\frac14 \min_{j\notin\{i, \phi_1(i) ,\dots, \phi_{p-1}(i)\}} |z_i-z_j|\)\vee \lambda,\end{equation}
where $\phi_0(i)=i$ and \edblue{for $1\le k\le2N-1$, $\phi_k(i)$ minimizes $|z_i-z_j|$ over $j\notin\{\phi_0(i),\ldots,\phi_{k-1}(i)\}$.} We call $z_{\phi_k(i)}$ the $k$-th nearest neighbor to $z_i$.
We note that we always have
\begin{equation*} \left(\frac 14 |z_i -z_{\phi_k(i)}|\right) \vee \lambda=  \rr_k(z_i).\end{equation*}

\subsubsection*{Nearest neighbor graphs}
As discussed in the introduction, the dipole decomposition  estimate will be used in conjunction with the method of Gunson-Panta \cite{GunPan} which breaks the configuration into nearest neighbor graphs. It is worth noting, however, that \cite{GunPan} builds the nearest neighbor graphs of all particles, irrespective of their sign, whereas for us the sign will  play an important role later.

The nearest-neighbor graph $\gamma_{2N}(Z_{2N})$ of $Z_{2N}$ is a directed graph  on $\{1,\ldots,2N\}$, with an edge from $p$ to $q$ if $z_q$ is the nearest-neighbor of $z_p$. \edblue{Connected components mean weakly connected components, obtained by forgetting edge orientations.} The graph $\gamma_{2N}(Z_{2N})$ has between $1$ and $N$ connected components and each of its connected components \edblue{contains exactly one directed $2$-cycle, with possibly empty trees directed toward its two vertices.} We denote by $D_{2N,K}$ the set of nearest-neighbor graphs on $\{1,\ldots,2N\}$ with $K$ connected components. \edblue{We retain the vertex labels.}

Let $\gamma\in D_{2N,K}$. Let us denote by $\mathcal{ I}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{ I}_K$ the connected components of $\gamma$ and for each $k\in \{1,\ldots,K\}$, let us label the two vertices of the 2-cycle in $\mathcal{ I}_k$ by $m_k$ and $m_k'$ and call $\mathcal C_k= \{m_k, m_k'\}$ the corresponding 2-cycle.
We also let
\begin{equation}
  \label{defIdip}
  \edblue{I^{\pair}:=\bigcup_{\mathcal I_k=\mathcal C_k}\mathcal C_k,
  \qquad I^{\dip}:=\bigcup_{\substack{\mathcal I_k=\mathcal C_k\\d_{m_k}d_{m_k'}=-1}}\mathcal C_k.}\end{equation}
$I^\pair$ corresponds to   points in a  2-cycle  which forms a connected component of the graph, i.e., has no tree attached. We call such pairs {\it isolated}.
Among the pairs we denote by  $I^\dip$ the indices corresponding to
isolated (neutral) dipoles.
We note that $\edblue{2(N-K)}$ bounds the number of connected components that are not reduced to a 2-cycle, i.e.,~have trees attached to them.

\begin{remark}\label{remark:maximal5}
  \edblue{Divide the plane about $O$ into six half-open cones of angle $\pi/3$. If $A,B\ne O$ lie in one cone and $|OA|\le|OB|$, then the cosine rule gives
  \[
    |AB|^2<|OA|^2+|OB|^2-|OA||OB|\le|OB|^2.
  \]
  Hence $O$ can be the first nearest neighbor of at most six points and the second nearest neighbor of at most twelve points. Indeed, three preimages in one cone would give the farthest point two neighbors closer than $O$.

  If we choose one vertex from each isolated pair, then at most six chosen vertices can have $O$ as second nearest neighbor. Indeed, two in one cone would give the farther vertex a point closer than $O$, distinct from its partner.}

\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Additional results on $\g_\lambda$}
\edblue{Set
\[
  u(x):=(\g*\delta_0^{(1)})(x)=
  \begin{cases}
    (1-|x|^2)/2,& |x|\le1,\\
    -\log|x|,& |x|\ge1.
  \end{cases}
\]
Its gradient is $1$-Lipschitz and $\dashint_{B(0,1)}\nab u=0$, where $\dashint$ denotes the average. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{idg1}
  \begin{aligned}
    \g_1(w)-\kappa
    &=\dashint_{B(0,1)}\bigl(u(x+w)-u(x)-\nab u(x)\cdot w\bigr)\,\dd x,\\
    |\g_1(w)-\kappa|&\le\hal|w|^2 \qquad\text{for every }w\in\R^2.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}}

\edblue{We have}
\begin{equation}\label{approxgeta} \g_\lambda(z)= \g(\lambda)+ \g_1(z/\lambda), \qquad
  |\g_\lambda(z)-\g(|z|\vee \lambda) |\le C,\end{equation}
where $C$ is some universal constant.
\edblue{The first identity follows by scaling. For the bound, write
\[
  \g_\lambda(z)-\g(|z|\vee\lambda)=H(z/\lambda),\qquad
  H(w):=\g_1(w)+\log(|w|\vee1).
\]
The function $H$ is continuous and vanishes for $|w|\ge2$ by Newton's theorem, hence is bounded.}

\subsection{Nearest neighbor expansion of the energy}
For $i\in \mathcal C_k$ for some $k=1, \dots, K$, we let
\begin{equation}\label{defD} D_i= d_i + d_{\phi_1(i)} .\end{equation}
If the $2$-cycle is neutral, as in the most typical case, then $D_i=0$.

The following proposition will be proven below.
\begin{prop}[Nearest neighbor decomposition of the energy]\label{prop:mino2}
  Let $(X_N, Y_N)$ be any configuration \edblue{satisfying the standing assumptions of this section} and consider its nearest neighbor graph decomposition as above. We have
  \begin{multline}
    \label{minoF02}
    \F_\lambda(X_N,Y_N) \\
    \ge \hal \sum_{k=1}^K \Bigg( \sum_{i\in \mathcal C_k} \edblue{\Bigl[}d_i d_{\phi_1(i)} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} ) -d_i D_i\(  \g\( \rr_2(z_i) \wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)} )  \)+\kappa\) -C \( \frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\)^2\edblue{\Bigr]}
    \\-\sum_{i\in \mathcal{I}_k \backslash \mathcal C_k} \(\g(\rr_2(z_i)) +\kappa \)\Bigg),
  \end{multline}
  where $C$ is universal.
\end{prop}
\edblue{The remainder $C(\frac{\rr_1}{\rr_2})^2$ measures the separation of a $2$-cycle from the remaining charges. For neutral cycles, it controls the interaction error associated with the dipoles.}
\edblue{For nonneutral cycles, combining the signed pair term with the $d_iD_i$ correction gives, relative to the neutral nearest-neighbor energy, at least
\[
  \log\frac{\rr_2(z_i)\wedge\rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})}{\rr_1(z_i)}-C
\]
per vertex, as shown below.}

We now rephrase this inequality into one that is less sharp but will be more convenient for our purposes and which will be the basis for our free energy upper bound.
\begin{coro}\label{coromino}
  For any configuration $Z_{2N}$ \edblue{satisfying the standing assumptions of this section}, using the above notation, we have
  \begin{multline}
    \label{minoF02 bis}
    \F_\lambda(Z_{2N})
    \ge - \hal \sum_{ i=1}^{2N} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} )+  \sum_{\substack{i\in I^{\pair}\backslash I^{\dip}\\ \phi_2(i) \in I^\pair, \phi_2(\phi_1(i)) \in I^\pair}}\( \log
    \frac{\rr_2(z_i) \wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})}{\rr_1(z_i)}-C\)
    \\
    -  C \sum_{i \in I^{\dip}, \phi_2(i)\in I^\pair , \phi_2(\phi_1(i)) \in I^\pair}
    \( \frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\)^2
    - C (N-K),
  \end{multline}where $C$ is universal, $I^\pair$ and $I^\dip$ are as in \eqref{defIdip} and $K$ is the number of components of the nearest neighbor graph.
\end{coro}
\edblue{The right-hand side is the nearest-neighbor energy $\F_\lambda^\nn := -\hal\sum_i\g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)})$ with three corrections: a logarithmic term for retained nonneutral pairs, a quadratic loss for retained neutral pairs, and the bulk loss $C(N-K)$. An isolated pair is retained when both endpoints have their second nearest neighbors in $I^\pair$.}
\begin{proof}[Proof of the corollary]
  \edblue{Each connected component contains exactly one $2$-cycle, so there are exactly $2N-2K$ tree vertices.}

  $\bullet$ The case $i\in \C_k$ and $d_id_{\phi_1(i)}=-1$. In that case we have $D_i=0$ and the corresponding term in the sum \eqref{minoF02} reduces to
  $$- \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}) - C  \( \frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\)^2 .$$ \edblue{There are $2(N-K)$ tree vertices and at most $2(N-K)$ nonisolated components, so $|(I^\pair)^c|\le6(N-K)$.} If $i\notin I^{\pair}$  then we can absorb the errors  $(\frac{\rr_1}{\rr_2})^2\le 1$ into $C(N-K)$.  Moreover,  by Remark \ref{remark:maximal5} a point can be the second nearest neighbor to at most \edblue{12} points. Hence, the number of $i$'s such that  $\phi_2(i) \notin I^\pair$ is bounded by $C(N-K)$, so for such $i$'s we can again absorb the error  $(\frac{\rr_1}{\rr_2})^2\le 1$ into $C(N-K)$. \edblue{We also discard their partners, which at most doubles this bound.}

  $\bullet$ The case $i\in \C_k$ and $d_i d_{\phi_1(i)}=1$. Then $d_i D_i= 2$. By definition of $\rr_2$ in \eqref{nn3} and the fact that $i\in \C_k$ and hence $z_i$ and $z_{\phi_1(i)}$ are each other's nearest neighbor,
  we have   $$\rr_2(z_i) \wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})\ge \rr_1(z_i)=\rr_1(z_{\phi_1(i)}) $$ and by definition \eqref{nn2} and \eqref{approxgeta}, we deduce that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{gr2r1}
    -\g(\rr_2(z_i)\wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)} ) )\ed{\ge -\g(\rr_1(z_i) )}\ge -\g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}) -C,
  \end{equation}
  for $C>0$ a universal constant. Thus, since $\rr_1\le \rr_2$,  we have
  \begin{multline*}\g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} ) -2 \(  \g\( \rr_2(z_i) \wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)} ) \)+\kappa\)- C \(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\)^2
    \\
    \ge - \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} ) -  2\g\(\frac{\rr_2(z_i) \wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})}{\rr_1(z_i)}\) - C,\end{multline*} for some universal constant $C$.
  Moreover,  $-  2\g\(\frac{\rr_2(z_i) \wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})}{\rr_1(z_i)}\) - C\ge - C$  since $\rr_2\ge \rr_1$, and thus,
  arguing as in the previous case, we may absorb such error terms into $C(N-K)$ \edblue{unless $i\in I^\pair$ and $\phi_2(i),\phi_2(\phi_1(i))\in I^\pair$.}

  $\bullet$ The case $\edblue{i\in\mathcal I_k\setminus\mathcal C_k}$.   In that case the term in the sum is just
  $$-\(\g(\rr_2(z_i)) +\kappa \)\ge - \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)})  \ed{-C}
  $$ after using that $\rr_2(z_i) \ge \rr_1(z_i)$ \ed{and again \eqref{nn2} and \eqref{approxgeta}. There are $2(N-K)$ tree vertices in $\bigcup_{k=1}^K(\mathcal I_k\setminus\mathcal C_k)$, so the constant error terms can again be absorbed into $C(N-K)$ as above.}
  Combining all the cases, we obtain the result.
\end{proof}

We now turn to the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:mino2}.
As explained in the introduction, the proof relies on an enlargement of the disc charges. To evaluate the change of energy, we use the following lemma.

\begin{lem}\label{lemchr} \edblue{For $\vec{\tau},\vec{\alpha}\in(0,\infty)^{2N}$, we have}
  \begin{equation}
    \label{llemchr}
    \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\R^2}\( |\nab h_{\vec{\tau}}|^2-|\nab h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2\) =\sum_{i,j} \int_{\R^2} d_i   d_j
    \(\g*\delta_{z_i}^{(\tau_i)} - \g* \delta_{z_i}^{(\alpha_i)} \)
    \( \delta_{z_j}^{(\tau_j)}+\delta_{z_j}^{(\alpha_j)}  \).\end{equation}

\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  \edblue{Set $u=h_{\vec\tau}-h_{\vec\alpha}$ and $v=h_{\vec\tau}+h_{\vec\alpha}$. By Newton's theorem, $u$ has compact support. Positive radii give local $C^{1,1}$ regularity, and neutrality gives $\nab h_{\vec\alpha},\nab h_{\vec\tau}=O(|x|^{-2})$ at infinity. Thus both gradients lie in $L^2(\R^2)$ and
  \[
    \int_{\R^2}\bigl(|\nab h_{\vec\tau}|^2-|\nab h_{\vec\alpha}|^2\bigr)
    =\int_{\R^2}\nab u\cdot\nab v
    =-\int_{\R^2}u\Delta v.
  \]
  The Poisson equations give \eqref{llemchr}.}
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:mino2}]
  We are going to define for each point $z_i$ in the configuration an appropriate radius $\tau_i$.
  Each index $i$ belongs to one connected component $\mathcal{I}_k$ of the nearest-neighbor graph $\gamma$ of the configuration.  We let
  \begin{equation}\label{deftau} \tau_i= \begin{cases}
      \rr_2(z_{i})\wedge  \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i) })  & \text{if $i\in \mathcal C_k$}\\
      \rr_2(z_i) & \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{equation}

  We  then apply Lemma \ref{lemchr} and  increase the balls  from $\alpha_i= \lambda $ to $\tau_i$.
  \edblue{The mixed interaction sums cancel by symmetry under $i\leftrightarrow j$, giving}
  \begin{multline}\label{peq}
    \int_{\R^2} |\nab h_{\lambda}|^2-
    \int_{\R^2}  |\nab h_{\vec{\tau}}|^2\\=
    2\pi \sum_{i,j} d_i d_j \(\iint \g(x-y) \delta_{z_i}^{(\lambda)} (x)
    \delta_{z_j}^{(\lambda)} (y)-  \iint \g(x-y) \delta_{z_i}^{(\tau_i)} (x)
    \delta_{z_j}^{(\tau_j)} (y)
    \)
    .    \end{multline}
  First, if $\tau_i=\tau_j=\lambda$, the terms in parentheses cancel. We may thus restrict the sum to the situation where $\max (\tau_i, \tau_j) > \lambda$, which also means that $\rr_2(z_i)$ or $\rr_2(z_j)$ is the true (quarter of the) second neighbor distance.
  Next, if $B(z_i, \lambda) $ and $B(z_j, \lambda)$ intersect, so do $B(z_i, \tau_i) $ and $B(z_j, \tau_j)$ since by definition and \eqref{nn3}, $\tau_i\ge \lambda$, $\tau_j \ge \lambda$.
  If, on the other hand, $B(z_i,\lambda)$ and $B(z_j, \lambda)$ are disjoint, and $B(z_i, \tau_i)$ and $B(z_j, \tau_j)$ as well, then
  by Newton's theorem and the mean value theorem, the two terms in parentheses are equal to $\g(z_i-z_j)$ and hence cancel.
  We may thus restrict the sum to the situation where $\max(\tau_i, \tau_j) > \lambda$ and $B(z_i, \tau_i)$ and $B(z_j, \tau_j)$ intersect, \edblue{hence}
  \begin{equation}\label{disz}
    |z_i-z_j|\le \tau_i+\tau_j \le \rr_2(z_i) + \rr_2(z_j) \le \edblue{2\bigl(\rr_2(z_i)\vee\rr_2(z_j)\bigr)} \end{equation} in view of the definitions \eqref{deftau}.

  \edblue{If $i\ne j$, the larger of $\rr_2(z_i),\rr_2(z_j)$ exceeds $\lambda$. By \eqref{disz}, the corresponding point has the other as its first nearest neighbor. Suppose that $j\ne\phi_1(i)$, so $i=\phi_1(j)$, and write $d=|z_i-z_j|$. Since $z_{\phi_1(i)}$ is distinct from $z_i,z_j$, the triangle inequality gives
  \[
    \rr_2(z_i)\le(d/4)\vee\lambda,
    \qquad \rr_2(z_j)\le(d/2)\vee\lambda.
  \]
  If $\lambda\ge d/2$, then $\tau_i=\tau_j=\lambda$, contrary to the growth condition. Otherwise $\rr_2(z_i)+\rr_2(z_j)<d$, contradicting \eqref{disz}. By symmetry, the sum reduces to $i=j$ or mutual nearest neighbors, which we denote by $i\sim j$. For a mutual-neighbor pair whose enlarged discs intersect, \eqref{disz} becomes
  \[
    |z_i-z_j|\le2\tau_i=2\bigl(\rr_2(z_i)\wedge\rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})\bigr).
  \]}

  \edblue{Terms with both radii unchanged or with disjoint enlarged discs vanish. We may therefore restore all mutual-neighbor pairs to the sum. Dropping $\int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec\tau}|^2\ge0$ in \eqref{peq} and using \eqref{eq:defgeta} and \eqref{geta0}, we obtain}
  \begin{multline}\label{trcomp}
    \int_{\R^2} |\nab h_{\lambda}|^2 \\
    \ge  2\pi \sum_{i=1}^{2N} \( \g_\lambda(0)  - \g(\tau_i)-\kappa \)+
    2\pi \sum_{i \neq j: j \sim i }
    \edblue{d_i d_j \Bigl(  \g_\lambda ( z_i-z_j)    -\iint \g(x-y) \delta_{z_i}^{(\tau_i)}(x) \delta_{z_j}^{(\tau_j)} (y)\Bigr)}.
  \end{multline}

  We  examine the contribution of the $2$-cycles. If $i\in \mathcal C_k$ and $j=\phi_1(i)$, then $\tau_i=\tau_j $ by definition and thus by \eqref{approxgeta} the contribution of the parenthesis in \eqref{trcomp} is
  $$\edblue{2\pi d_id_j\Bigl( \g_\lambda(z_i-z_j)- \g(\tau_i)  - \g_1(\frac{z_i-z_j}{\tau_i})\Bigr)} .$$
  This term appears twice due to the two edges between the vertices of the cycle and to the equality $\tau_i=\tau_j$.

  If $i\sim j$, \ed{then, considering $z_k$ a ``second nearest neighbor'' to $z_i$, i.e.~achieving the min in \eqref{nn3},  by the triangle inequality}    we have
  \begin{equation}\label{ppj}\rr_2 (z_j) \le \frac14|z_i-z_j|+\rr_2(z_i).\end{equation}
  Reversing the roles of $i$ and $j$, we obtain, for $i \sim j$,
  \begin{equation}\label{rrj}|\rr_2(z_i)-\rr_2(z_j)|\le \frac14|z_i-z_j| \le \rr_{1} (z_i) . \end{equation}
  \edblue{Writing $d=|z_i-z_j|$, \eqref{rrj} gives
  \[
    \rr_1(z_i)\le\tau_i,\qquad
    \rr_2(z_i)\le\tau_i+d/4\le2\tau_i,\qquad
    d\le4\rr_1(z_i).
  \]
  By \eqref{idg1},
  \[
    E_i:=\kappa-\g_1\left(\frac{z_i-z_j}{\tau_i}\right),\qquad
    |E_i|\le\frac{d^2}{2\tau_i^2}
    \le32\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\right)^2.
  \]}
  Inserting these facts into \eqref{trcomp}, we obtain that
  \begin{multline}\label{trcomp b}
    \int_{\R^2} |\nab h_{\lambda}|^2
    \ge  4\pi N \g_\lambda(0)  -  2\pi
    \sum_{i=1}^{2N} ( \g(\tau_i)+\kappa)\\+
    2\pi \sum_{i\neq j:j \sim i }
    d_i d_j \Bigg(  \g_\lambda ( z_i-z_j)    - \g(\tau_i) -\kappa + \edblue{E_i} \Bigg).
  \end{multline}
  \edblue{Since $d_id_j=\pm1$, we have $d_id_jE_i\ge-C(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)})^2$. Regrouping over the components gives}
  \begin{multline*}
    \int_{\R^2} |\nab h_{\lambda}|^2
    \ge  4\pi N \g_\lambda(0) \\ +
    2\pi\sum_{k=1}^K \Bigg( \sum_{i\in \mathcal C_k} \edblue{\Bigl[}d_i d_{\phi_1(i)} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} ) -d_i \( d_i+ d_{\phi_1(i)} \)(  \g(\tau_i) +\kappa) \edblue{-C\Bigl(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\Bigr)^2\Bigr]}
    \\
    -\sum_{i\in \mathcal{I}_k \backslash \mathcal C_k} \edblue{\bigl(\g(\rr_2(z_i))+\kappa\bigr)} \Bigg).
  \end{multline*}
  In view of  the  rewriting  of $\F_\lambda$ \eqref{eq:rewrF}  we obtain the result.

\end{proof}

\section{Free energy upper bound}\label{sec3}

This section is devoted to the proof of the free energy upper bound.
This will be based on the energy lower bound of Corollary \ref{coromino} which reduces the interaction to nearest neighbor terms, together with a quadratic error depending on second nearest neighbor distances. The main difficulty is to partition the phase-space efficiently to integrate the exponential of this reduced energy. This is based on refinements of the Gunson-Panta change of variables and approach \cite{GunPan}.
We will start by proving  upper bounds for the simpler nearest neighbor model $\F_\lambda^\nn$, then for simpler models with errors,  and build up to the upper bound for the full model including the second nearest neighbor errors.
\edblue{Throughout this section, $N\ge2$ and the estimates as $\lambda\to0$ are uniform in $N$.}

\subsection{Preliminaries: \texorpdfstring{\edgreen{Dirichlet integrals and Gaussian decoupling}}{Dirichlet integrals and Gaussian decoupling}}
We start by presenting some tools needed to implement the integrations.
\subsubsection*{Dirichlet integrals}We first recall a result on computing ``multiple Dirichlet integrals of type 1" (see \cite[p. 258]{whittaker_watson_1996}).
\begin{lem}[Dirichlet integrals]\label{lemdirichlet}
  Given an integer $k\geq 1$, $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k>0$ and $s>0$, let
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:indis}
    I_{k,s}(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_k) :=\int_{(\R^+)^k} \indic_{0<t_1+\ldots+t_k<s}\, t_1^{\alpha_1-1}\ldots t_k^{\alpha_k-1}\dd t_1\ldots \dd t_k.
  \end{equation}
  We have
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ind I}
    I_{k,s}(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_k) =s^{\alpha_1+\dots+ \alpha_k}  \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_1)\ldots \Gamma(\alpha_k)}{\Gamma(\alpha_1+\ldots+\alpha_k)}\frac{1}{\alpha_1+\ldots+\alpha_k}.
  \end{equation}

  \edblue{For $k_0\in\{0,\ldots,k\}$,} if the $k$-tuple $(\alpha_i)_{i=1}^k$ is defined by $\alpha_i=\alpha>0$ for $1\le i\le k_0$ and $\alpha_i=1$ for $k_0+ 1\le i \le k$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:estimate Dirichlet}
    \log I_{k,s}=(\alpha k_0+(k-k_0))\( \log s- \log(\alpha k_0+k-k_0)\)+k+\edblue{O_\alpha(k_0+\log(k+1))}.
  \end{equation}
  \edblue{Moreover,}
  \begin{equation} I_{k,s}\le \( \frac{s}{\alpha k_0 +k-k_0} \)^{\alpha k_0+k-k_0}e^k \edblue{C_\alpha^{k_0}}.\end{equation}
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  The identity
  \begin{equation}\int_{(\R^+)^k} \indic_{0<t_1+\ldots+t_k<1 }\ t_1^{\alpha_1-1}\ldots t_k^{\alpha_k-1}\dd t_1\ldots \dd t_k
    = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_1)\ldots \Gamma(\alpha_k)}{\Gamma(\alpha_1+\ldots+\alpha_k)}\frac{1}{\alpha_1+\ldots+\alpha_k}\end{equation}
  \edblue{follows by successive applications of Euler's beta integral.}
  It follows by the change of variables $t_i=s u_i$ that \eqref{eq:ind I} holds.

  For the particular choice of $\alpha_i$'s stated in the lemma, we thus find  that
  \begin{equation*}
    I_{k,s}=s^{ \alpha k_0+k-k_0}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)^{k_0}}{\Gamma(\alpha k_0+k-k_0)}\frac{1}{\alpha k_0+k-k_0}.
  \end{equation*}

  Applying Stirling's formula, we obtain
  \begin{multline*}\log I_{k,s}= (\alpha k_0+k-k_0)\log s+ k_0\log \Gamma(\alpha) \\ - (\alpha k_0+k-k_0) \log (\alpha k_0+k-k_0)+ (\alpha k_0+k-k_0)+\edblue{O_\alpha(\log(k+1))}.\end{multline*}

  The relation \eqref{eq:estimate Dirichlet} follows. \edblue{We deduce the upper bound from $\Gamma(x+1)\ge(x/e)^x$ for $x>0$.}\end{proof}



\subsubsection*{Gunson-Panta change of variables}
The Gunson-Panta method relies on considering the nearest neighbor graph of a configuration and partitioning the phase-space accordingly. We use the notation  introduced already in Section \ref{sec2}, but we can apply it more generally to $p$ points, and not necessarily an even number of points.
\edblue{For $p\ge2$ distinct points $z_1,\ldots,z_p\in\Lambda$ with unique nearest neighbors,} let $\mathcal{I}_1, \dots , \mathcal{I}_K$ denote the connected components of \edblue{their nearest-neighbor graph}, and denote by $m_k$ and $m_k'$ the indices of the two vertices of the 2-cycle $\mathcal C_k$. \edblue{On each graph, choose $m_k'=\max\mathcal C_k$ and set
\[
  \Omega_\gamma:=\{Z_p\in\Lambda^p:\gamma_p(Z_p)=\gamma\}.
\]}
We then define the Gunson-Panta change of variables via the map
\begin{equation}\label{defphi}
  \Phi^{GP}_p (z_1, \dots, z_p) = (u_1, \dots, u_p),\end{equation} where
\begin{equation}
  u_i=\left\{
  \begin{array}{ll}
    z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} & \text{if }i\in \mathcal{I}_k,i\neq m_k' \\
    z_{i} & \text{if } i=m_k'.
  \end{array}
  \right.
\end{equation}
\edblue{Deleting the outgoing edge of each $m_k'$ leaves a rooted tree in each component. Ordering parents before children makes the map triangular with identity diagonal blocks. Hence $\Phi_p^{GP}$ is invertible on $\Omega_\gamma$ and $|\det D\Phi_p^{GP}|=1$.}



\edgreen{\subsubsection*{Gaussian decoupling of packing constraints}
After the Gunson--Panta change of variables described above, the integrands we will need to study are  products of radial weights in the Gunson--Panta variables $u_j$, but the integration domain is not a product: the discs $B(z_i,|z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}|/2)$ are disjoint and contained in $[-\sqrt N,2\sqrt N]^2$, so the variables $u_j$ satisfy a packing constraint $\sum_j|u_j|^2\le CN$. Lemma~\ref{lemdirichlet} computed such constrained integrals exactly for power weights. For the other weights, we decouple the constraint by an exponential Markov bound.

\begin{lem}[Gaussian decoupling]\label{lem:gaussian decoupling}
  Let $k\ge1$, $s>0$, $a>0$, and let $w_1,\ldots,w_k:\R^2\to[0,\infty)$ be measurable. Then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-decoupling-lemma}
    \int_{(\R^2)^k}\indic_{\sum_{j=1}^k|u_j|^2\le s}\prod_{j=1}^kw_j(u_j)\dd u_1\ldots\dd u_k
    \le e^{as}\prod_{j=1}^k\int_{\R^2}w_j(u)e^{-a|u|^2}\dd u.
  \end{equation}
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
  Integrate the pointwise bound
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-decoupling}
    \indic_{\sum_j|u_j|^2\le s}\le e^{a(s-\sum_j|u_j|^2)}
  \end{equation}
  and use Fubini's theorem.
\end{proof}

The cost of the decoupling is the factor $e^{as}$, and its effect is to replace the constraint by a Gaussian cutoff at scale $a^{-1/2}$ on each variable. Each weight then becomes integrable at infinity. The bound is essentially sharp when $a$ is chosen well. For instance, for the power weights of Lemma~\ref{lemdirichlet}, written in the variables $t_j=|u_j|^2$ as $t_j^{\alpha-1}$ with $\alpha>0$, the choice $a=\alpha k/s$ gives
\[
  e^{as}\Bigl(\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}e^{-at}\dd t\Bigr)^k
  =\Gamma(\alpha)^k\Bigl(\frac{es}{\alpha k}\Bigr)^{\alpha k},
\]
which exceeds the exact value $\Gamma(\alpha)^ks^{\alpha k}/\Gamma(\alpha k+1)$ of \eqref{eq:ind I} only by the factor $(e/\alpha k)^{\alpha k}\Gamma(\alpha k+1)=\sqrt{2\pi\alpha k}\,(1+o(1))$, by Stirling's formula. In general, $a$ should be of the order of the number of variables divided by $s$, that is, of the inverse of the typical value of $|u_j|^2$ allowed by the constraint.

In Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk} below, for every $\beta\ge0$, the Gaussian factor $e^{-c\sum_i r(z_i)^2}$ is produced either by the excluded volume of the trivial components, at no cost, or by Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian decoupling} with $a$ of order $1$, at cost $e^{CN}\le e^{Ck}$. Propositions~\ref{prop:two species} and~\ref{prop:isolated} reuse this integration. In these statements, errors of size $C_\beta k$ are allowed, so the precise value of $a$ is irrelevant.

The choice of $a$ matters only for the weight $e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}$, that is, for $2$-cycles at $\beta=2$ and for tree edges at $\beta=4$. Since $\g_\lambda(u)=-\log|u|$ for $|u|\ge2\lambda$, this weight is $|u|^{-2}$ outside the core. Each dyadic annulus then carries the same mass $2\pi\log2$, and the integral over $\R^2$ diverges logarithmically. The smearing cuts the divergence at scale $\lambda$, and the packing constraint cuts it at scale of order $1$. The scales between $\lambda$ and $1$ give the mass $2\pi|\log\lambda|$. For $2$-cycles at $\beta=2$, this is the origin of the term $\log|\log\lambda|$ in \eqref{eq:devlogz}, with $\mc Z_2=2\pi$. By contrast, for $\beta>2$ the cycle weight $e^{\beta\g_\lambda}$ is integrable on $\R^2$, with mass $\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}$ concentrated at scale $\lambda$, and no constraint is needed (see \eqref{3.20b} below). For tree edges with $\beta<4$, the weight $|u|^{-\beta/2}$ has a mass that is polynomial in the cutoff, and Lemma~\ref{lemdirichlet} computes the constrained integral exactly.

For the weight $e^{2\g_\lambda}$, the mass depends on the cutoff at leading order. With \eqref{eq:gaussian-decoupling}, the cutoff moves from scale $1$ to scale $a^{-1/2}$, which adds $\pi\log(1/a)$ to the mass $2\pi|\log\lambda|$. With $s=CN$ and $N$ pairs, the total error is of order
\[
  N\Bigl(a+\frac{\log(1/a)}{|\log\lambda|}\Bigr).
\]
The choice $a=1/|\log\lambda|$ makes it $O(N(1+\log|\log\lambda|)/|\log\lambda|)$. This is the choice made in Lemma~\ref{lemma:part nn} and in the proofs of \eqref{eq:critical upper} and \eqref{eq:critical electric}. Lemma~\ref{lemma:nearest neigh}, at $\beta=2$ and $\beta=4$, uses the same balance with $K$ cycles or $2p-2K$ tree edges in place of $N$ pairs. The next lemma collects the integrals of the weight $e^{2\g_\lambda}$ used in these proofs.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:critical radial}
  Let $0<\lambda\le e^{-1}$. There is a universal constant $C>0$ such that the following hold.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For $a>0$ with $a\lambda^2\le1/4$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-gaussian}
      J_\lambda(a):=\int_{\R^2}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)-a|u|^2}\dd u
      =\pi\log\frac1{a\lambda^2}+O(1).
    \end{equation}
    \item We have     \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-B}
      \edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}=2\pi\Bigl(\ell+\frac12\log\ell\Bigr)+O(1),\qquad
      \log \edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}\le\log(2\pi\ell)+C\frac{1+\log\ell}{\ell}.
    \end{equation}
    For $2\lambda\le R\le1$, \edgreen{the part of $J_\lambda(1/\ell)$ carried by $|u|\ge R$} satisfies
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-BR}
      \edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell;R}):=\int_{|u|\ge R}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)-|u|^2/\ell}\dd u
      =2\pi\Bigl(\log\frac1R+\frac12\log\ell\Bigr)+O(1).
    \end{equation}
    \item For $\rho\ge\lambda$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-disc}
      \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}\dd u=2\pi(\ell+\log\rho)+O(1).
    \end{equation}
    \item For every $C_1>0$, there is $C_2>0$ such that, for $\rho\ge\lambda$ and $a(u):=(|u|/4)\vee\lambda$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-correction}
      \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}\Bigl(e^{C_1(a(u)/\rho)^2}-1\Bigr)\dd u\le C_2,
      \qquad
      \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}\Bigl(\frac{a(u)}{\rho}\Bigr)^4\dd u\le C_2.
    \end{equation}
  \end{enumerate}
  The $O(1)$ terms are bounded by $C$ in absolute value.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
  By \eqref{approxgeta}, $\g_\lambda(u)\le\g(|u|\vee\lambda)+C\le\ell+C$ on $B(0,2\lambda)$, so the core satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-core}
    0\le\int_{|u|<2\lambda}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}\dd u\le4\pi e^{2C}.
  \end{equation}
  By Newton's theorem, $\g_\lambda(u)=-\log|u|$ for $|u|\ge2\lambda$. Let $E_1(x):=\int_x^\infty e^{-t}t^{-1}\dd t$. For $0<x\le1$, the bounds $|e^{-t}-1|\le t$ and $\int_1^\infty e^{-t}t^{-1}\dd t\le e^{-1}$ give
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:E1}
    \Bigl|E_1(x)-\log\frac1x\Bigr|\le1.
  \end{equation}

  (1) In polar coordinates and with $t=ar^2$, the part $|u|\ge2\lambda$ of $J_\lambda(a)$ equals
  \[
    2\pi\int_{2\lambda}^\infty\frac{e^{-ar^2}}{r}\dd r=\pi E_1(4a\lambda^2).
  \]
  Since $4a\lambda^2\le1$, \eqref{eq:E1} and \eqref{eq:critical-core} give \eqref{eq:critical-gaussian}.

  (2) Since $\lambda^2/\ell\le e^{-2}<1/4$, item (1) applies with $a=1/\ell$ and gives $\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}=\pi\log(\ell/\lambda^2)+O(1)=2\pi\ell+\pi\log\ell+O(1)$. Hence $\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}\le2\pi\ell\,(1+(\log\ell+C)/(2\ell))$, and $\log(1+x)\le x$ gives the bound on $\log \edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}$. Since $R\ge2\lambda$, the integrand of $\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell;R})$ is $|u|^{-2}e^{-|u|^2/\ell}$, so $\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell;R})=\pi E_1(R^2/\ell)$. Since $R^2/\ell\le1$, \eqref{eq:E1} gives \eqref{eq:critical-BR}.

  (3) Since $4\rho\ge2\lambda$, the annulus $2\lambda\le|u|\le4\rho$ contributes $2\pi\log(2\rho/\lambda)=2\pi(\ell+\log\rho)+2\pi\log2$, and \eqref{eq:critical-core} bounds the core.

  (4) For $|u|\le4\rho$, we have $a(u)\le\rho$, and $e^{C_1x^2}-1\le(e^{C_1}-1)x^2$ for $0\le x\le1$, by convexity. It therefore suffices to bound $\int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}(a(u)/\rho)^2\dd u$, which also dominates the second integral. On the core, $(a/\rho)^2\le1$, and \eqref{eq:critical-core} applies. For $|u|\ge2\lambda$, we have $a(u)\le|u|/2$, so the annulus contributes at most
  \[
    \int_{2\lambda\le|u|\le4\rho}\frac1{|u|^2}\frac{|u|^2}{4\rho^2}\dd u\le\frac{\pi(4\rho)^2}{4\rho^2}=4\pi.
  \]
\end{proof}}

\subsection{Upper bound for a nearest neighbor model}

Corollary \ref{coromino} tells us that, up to an error term involving ratios of nearest and second-nearest neighbor distances, one can bound $\F_\lambda$ from below by  $\F_\lambda^\nn(Z_{2N})$ where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defdipoleen}
  \F_\lambda^\nn(Z_{2p}):=  - \hal \sum_{ i=1}^{2p}
  \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} ).
\end{equation}
We examine in the next two lemmas the nearest neighbor model studied in \cite{GunPan} and the reduced dipole model. The proofs will be a model for what follows.

\begin{lem}[\edblue{Upper bounds for the nearest-neighbor model on a fixed graph}]\label{lemma:nearest neigh}
  Let $\beta\in [2,+\infty)$ and $\mathcal Z_\beta$ be the constant defined in \eqref{def:Cbeta}. Let $p\in \{1,\ldots,N\}$, $K\in \{1,\ldots,p\}$ and $\gamma\in D_{2p,K}$. \edblue{The bounds below hold uniformly in $N\ge2$ for $0<\lambda<\lambda_\beta$, where $\lambda_\beta\in(0,e^{-2}]$ depends only on $\beta$.}

  $\bullet$ For $\beta=2$ we have
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:est2}
    \log \int_{\Omega_\gamma} \exp\(-\beta \F_\lambda^{\nn}(Z_{2p})\)  \dd Z_{2p} \leq K \log N
    \\+ K\log \Bigl( |\log \lambda|+\log \frac{N}{K}\Bigr)+K\log 2\pi +\Bigl(1-\frac{\beta}{4}\Bigr)(2p-2K)\log \frac{N}{p-K}+C_\beta(p-K) +O\(\frac{N \log \log \frac1\lambda}{\log\frac1 \lambda}\).
  \end{multline}}

  $\bullet$ For $\beta\in (2,4)$, we have
  \edblue{\begin{multline}
    \log \int_{\Omega_\gamma} \exp\(-\beta \F_\lambda^{\nn}(Z_{2p})\)  \dd Z_{2p} \\ \leq K \log N
    + (2-\beta)K\log\lambda+K \log \mathcal Z_{\beta}+\Bigl(1-\frac{\beta}{4}\Bigr)(2p-2K)\log \frac{N}{p-K}+C_\beta(p-K).
  \end{multline}}

  $\bullet$ For $\beta=4$, we have
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:estb4}
    \log \int_{\Omega_\gamma} \exp\(-\beta \F_\lambda^{\nn}(Z_{2p})\)  \dd Z_{2p} \leq K \log N
    \\ +(2-\beta)K\log \lambda+K\log \mc{Z}_\beta+  (2p-2K)\log\Bigl(|\log \lambda|+\log \frac{N}{p-K}\Bigr)+C_\beta(p-K).
  \end{multline}}

  $\bullet$ For $\beta>4$, we have
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:estb4 bis}
    \log \int_{\Omega_\gamma} \exp\(-\beta \F_\lambda^{\nn}(Z_{2p})\)  \dd Z_{2p}
    \leq K \log N  \\   +(2-\beta)K\log \lambda+K\log \mc{Z}_\beta+(2p-2K)\Bigl(2-\frac{\beta}{2}\Bigr)\log \lambda+C_\beta(p-K).
  \end{multline}}
  \edblue{Terms proportional to $p-K$ are zero when $p=K$.}
\end{lem}

Because $\beta\geq 2$, the \edblue{free energy of a neutral dipole} diverges like $\log(\lambda^{2-\beta}\indic_{\beta>2}+|\log \lambda|\indic_{\beta=2})$ as $\lambda$ tends to $0$. \edblue{This divergence favors short pairs. The predominance of neutral pairs requires the signed-energy correction and the entropy estimates below. Their lengths are controlled in Theorem~\ref{theorem:th1}.}

\begin{proof}{\bf Step 1: change of variables.}
  Let $\gamma\in D_{2p,K}$. As in Section 2, let us denote by $\mathcal{I}_k$, $k=1,\ldots,K$, the connected components of $\gamma$ and $\mc{C}_k:=\{m_k,m_k'\}\subset \mathcal{I}_k$ the $2$-cycle in each connected component.

  For brevity, let us denote
  \begin{equation*}
    J(\gamma):=\int_{\edblue{\Omega_\gamma}}\exp\(-\beta \F_\lambda^{\nn}(Z_{2p})\)\dd Z_{2p},
  \end{equation*}
  where again $\Lambda= [0, \sqrt N]^2$. Performing the Gunson-Panta change of variables \eqref{defphi}, we may write
  \edblue{\begin{equation}\label{eq:red neutral}
    J(\gamma)
    =\int_{\edblue{\Phi^{GP}_{2p}(\Omega_\gamma)}}\prod_{k=1}^K \edblue{\Bigg[\exp(\beta\g_\lambda(u_{m_k}))}\prod_{\substack{i\in \mathcal{I}_k\backslash \mathcal C_k }} \exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_{\lambda}(u_i)\Bigr)\edblue{\Bigg]}\dd U_{2p}.
  \end{equation}}
  Indeed, if $\{m_k,m_k'\}$ is a $2$-cycle, the interaction $\g_\lambda(u_{m_k})$ is counted twice, which explains why this term appears in the above integral with a factor $\beta$ instead of $\beta/2$. This turns out to be crucial:
  since $\beta\geq 2$, the integral of the $2$-cycle factor $\exp(\beta \g_\lambda(u_{m_k}))$ diverges as $\lambda\to0$. On the other hand   the weight of pairs of points which are not in a 2-cycle gives \edgreen{a locally integrable weight, uniformly in $\lambda$,} if $\beta<4$\edgreen{, and a weight whose integral near the origin diverges as $\lambda\to0$} if $\beta \ge 4$.
  \smallskip

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 2: separation of variables.}
  The domain of integration in \eqref{eq:red neutral} is a complicated subset of $\R^{4p}$ but one can approximate it by only keeping the volume constraint:
  \edblue{\begin{equation*}
    \edblue{\Phi^{GP}_{2p}(\Omega_\gamma)}\subset B:=\Bigl\{U_{2p}\in \R^{4p}: \sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in \mathcal{I}_k,i\neq m_k'} |u_i|^2 \leq \frac{ 36 N}{\pi}, \forall k\in \{1,\ldots,K\}, u_{m_k'}\in \Lambda \Bigr\}.
  \end{equation*}}
  This takes advantage of the fact that the balls $B(z_i, \frac{|z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}|}{2})$ are disjoint balls in $ [-\sqrt N, 2 \sqrt N]^2$.

  Splitting the variables into two categories (those corresponding to $i =m_k'$, i.e.~the reference points, and the others) and integrating a reference point in each connected component in the box $\Lambda$ of volume $N$, we find
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:cutt}
    \int_{B}\prod_{k=1}^K \edblue{\Bigg[\exp(\beta\g_\lambda(u_{m_k}))}\prod_{i\in \mathcal{I}_k \backslash \mathcal C_k  } \exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_i) \Bigr)\edblue{\Bigg]}\dd U_{2p}\\ \leq \Bigl(\int_{B_1}
    \prod_{k=1}^K \exp(\beta \g_\lambda(u_{m_k}))
    \dd u_{m_k}\Bigr) N^K
    \times\int_{B_2}\exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_1)\Bigr)\ldots \exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_{2p-2K})\Bigr)\dd U_{2p-2K},
  \end{multline}}
  where
  \edblue{\begin{equation}\label{def:D1}
    B_1:=\Bigl\{U_{K}\in (\dR^2)^K:|U_{K}|^2\leq \edblue{\frac{36N}{\pi}}\Bigr\},
  \end{equation}}
  \edblue{\begin{equation}\label{def:D2}
    B_2:=\Bigl\{U_{2p-2K}\in (\R^2)^{2p-2K}:|U_{2p-2K}|^2\leq \frac{ 36 N}{\pi}\Bigr\}.
  \end{equation}}
  \edgreen{In the last integral of \eqref{eq:cutt}, we have relabeled the tree-edge variables $u_i$, $i\in\bigcup_k(\mathcal I_k\setminus\mathcal C_k)$, as $u_1,\ldots,u_{2p-2K}$.}

  \noindent
  {\bf{Step 3: integration of the $2$-cycle distances.}}

  $\bullet$ The case $\beta>2$. Performing a polar change of coordinates and using \eqref{approxgeta}, we get
  \begin{equation}\label{3.20b}
    \int_{\R^2}\exp(\beta \g_\lambda(u))\dd u=2\pi\int_0^{\infty}\exp(\beta \g_\lambda(r))r\dd r=2\pi\lambda^{2-\beta}\int_0^{\infty}\exp(\beta \g_1(r))r\dd r=\lambda^{2-\beta}\mathcal Z_{\beta}>0
  \end{equation}
  by definition of $\mc{Z}_\beta$, see \eqref{def:Cbeta}. Hence, removing the restriction to the set $B_1$, we obtain
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:dipa}
    \log\int_{B_1} \prod_{k=1}^K \exp(\beta \g_\lambda(u_{m_k}))\dd u_{m_k}\leq K\log (\lambda^{2-\beta}\mc{Z}_\beta).
  \end{equation}

  $\bullet$ The case $\beta=2$. \edblue{\edgreen{Here the cycle weight $e^{2\g_\lambda}$ is not integrable on $\R^2$, and we use Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian decoupling} as described before Lemma~\ref{lem:critical radial}.}
  \edgreen{Set} $s=36N/\pi$, $L=\ell+\log(N/K)$ and $a=K/(sL)$. \edgreen{The constraint $\sum_k|u_{m_k}|^2\le s$ allows each of the $K$ cycles a length of order $(s/K)^{1/2}$. The Gaussian cutoff $a^{-1/2}=(sL/K)^{1/2}$ is slightly larger, so that the decoupling costs only $e^{as}=e^{K/L}$. We have $a\lambda^2\le\lambda^2/\ell\le1/4$, so \eqref{eq:critical-gaussian} applies. Since $\log(s/K)\le\log(N/K)+C$, it gives
  \[
    J_\lambda(a)\le\pi\Bigl(2\ell+\log\frac NK+\log L\Bigr)+C\le2\pi L+\pi\log L+C.
  \]
  } Using $\indic_{\sum_j|u_j|^2\le s}\le e^{a(s-\sum_j|u_j|^2)}$, we obtain
  \begin{align*}
    \log\int_{B_1}\prod_{k=1}^K e^{2\g_\lambda(u_{m_k})}\dd u_{m_k}
    &\le as+K\log J_\lambda(a)\\
    &\le K\log(2\pi)+K\log L+CK\frac{1+\log L}{L}.
  \end{align*}
  Since $K\le N$ and $L\ge\ell\ge2$, the error is at most $CN(1+\log\ell)/\ell$.}

  \noindent
  {\bf{Step 4: Integration of the other distances and conclusion.}}
  \edblue{For $p=K$, the $B_2$ integral is~$1$. We assume $p>K$ in this step.}

  $\bullet$ The case $\beta\in [2,4)$. For $i\in \{1,\ldots,2p-2K\}$, the unregularized weight $\exp(\frac{\beta}{2}\g(u_i))$ is locally integrable, and therefore the cutoff $\lambda$ can be removed. Using that $\g_\lambda\leq \g+C$ from \eqref{approxgeta} we find
  \edblue{\begin{multline*}
    \int_{B_2}\exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_1)\Bigr)\cdots \exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_{2p-2K})\Bigr)\dd U_{2p-2K}\\ \leq C_\beta^{2p-2K}\int_{B_2}\frac{1}{|u_1|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}\cdots \frac{1}{|u_{2p-2K}|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}\dd U_{2p-2K}.
  \end{multline*}}
  By performing again a polar change of coordinates  and the change of variables $r_i'= r_i^2$, we can rewrite this as an integral over a simplex of $\R^{2p-2K}$:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:reD}
    \int_{B_2}\frac{1}{|u_1|^{\frac{\beta}{2}} }\cdots \frac{1}{|u_{2p-2K}|^{\frac{\beta}{2} } }\dd U_{2p-2K}\edblue{=\pi^{2p-2K}} \int_{B_2'}{r_1^{-\frac{\beta}{4}}}\ldots{r_{2p-2K}^{-\frac{\beta}{4}} }\dd R_{2p-2K},
  \end{equation}
  where
  \begin{equation*}
    B_2':=\left\{R_{2p-2K}\in (\R^{+})^{2p-2K}: r_1+\ldots+r_{2p-2K}\leq \frac{36 N}{\pi}\right\}.
  \end{equation*}

  Since $\beta\in [2,4)$, we can set $\alpha=1-\frac{\beta}{4}>0$ and $k=k_0=2p-2K$ and insert \eqref{eq:estimate Dirichlet} with $ s= \frac{36 N}{\pi}$  into \eqref{eq:reD} to obtain
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:bound dir}
    \log \int_{B_2}
    \frac{1}{|u_1|^{\frac{\beta}{2}} }\cdots \frac{1}{|u_{2p-2K}|^{\frac{\beta}{2} } }\dd U_{2p-2K}
    \\\leq \Bigl(1-\frac{\beta}{4}\Bigr) (2p-2K) \log N  -\Bigl(1-\frac{\beta}{4}\Bigr)   (2p- 2K)\log\Bigl(\Bigl(1-\frac\beta4\Bigr) (2p-2K)\Bigr)+C_\beta(p-K)\\
    = \Bigl( 1-\frac\beta4\Bigr)(2p-2K)\log \frac{N}{p-K}+C_\beta(p-K).
  \end{multline}}

  $\bullet$ The case $\beta=4$. \edblue{We use \eqref{eq:critical-gaussian} with $a=(2p-2K)/(sL)$, where $s=36N/\pi$ and $L=|\log\lambda|+\log(N/(p-K))$. The same Gaussian bound gives}
  \edblue{\begin{multline*}
    \log \int_{B_2}\exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_1)\Bigr)\cdots \exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_{2p-2K})\Bigr)\dd U_{2p-2K}\leq (2p-2K)\log\Bigl(|\log \lambda|+\log \frac{N}{p-K}\Bigr)
    \\+C_\beta(p-K).
  \end{multline*}}

  $\bullet$ The case $\beta>4$. Arguing as in \eqref{3.20b}, one can check that
  \edblue{\begin{equation*}
    \log \int_{B_2}\exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_1)\Bigr)\cdots \exp\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{2}\g_\lambda(u_{2p-2K})\Bigr)\dd U_{2p-2K}\leq (2p-2K)\Bigl(2-\frac{\beta}{2}\Bigr)\log \lambda+C_\beta(p-K).
  \end{equation*}}

  Inserting the estimates of Steps 3 and 4 into \eqref{eq:cutt} proves the result.

\end{proof}

\edblue{We now expand the partition function of the reduced dipole model by summing over the neutral $2$-cycles.}

\begin{lem}[Free energy of the reduced dipole model]\label{lemma:part nn}
  \edblue{Fix $\beta\ge2$. The expansion below holds uniformly in $N\ge2$ for $0<\lambda<\lambda_\beta$, where $\lambda_\beta\in(0,e^{-2}]$ depends only on $\beta$.}
  Let us define
  \begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\F}_\lambda(Z_{2N}):= -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2N} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}) \indic_{\phi_1\circ\phi_1(i)=i, d_i d_{\phi_1(i) }=-1}
  \end{equation*}
  and
  \begin{equation*}
    \K_{N,\beta}^\lambda:=\int_{\Lambda^{2N} } \exp(-\beta \tilde{\F}_\lambda(Z_{2N}))\dd Z_{2N}.
  \end{equation*}
  \edblue{We have $0<\K_{N,\beta}^\lambda<\infty$ and}
  \begin{multline*}
    \log \K_{N,\beta}^\lambda=2N\log N+N\Big((2-\beta) (\log \lambda) \indic_{\beta>2}+(\log|\log\lambda|)\indic_{\beta=2}+\log \mc{Z}_\beta-1\Big)\\
    +O_\beta(N\bar \omega_\lambda)\edblue{+O_\beta(\log(N+1))},
  \end{multline*}
  where
  \begin{equation}\label{def:o nn}
    \bar \omega_\lambda=\begin{cases}
      |\log\lambda|^{-\nicefrac{1}{2}} & \text{if $\beta=2$}\\
      \edblue{\lambda^{\min\{(\beta-2)/2,1\}}} & \text{if $\beta>2$}.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
\end{lem}
Note that the expansion of $\log \K_{N,\beta}^\lambda$ is the same as \eqref{eq:devlogz} up to the error terms.
\smallskip

\begin{proof}
  \edblue{We obtain the upper bound by summing over the neutral $2$-cycles, and the lower bound by restricting to configurations of $N$ short neutral pairs. First assume  $\beta=2$. 
  For a configuration, let $\mc M$ be the set of pairs $\{x_i,y_j\}$ which are mutual nearest neighbors. Since nearest neighbors are unique, these pairs are disjoint, so $\mc M$ is a partial matching between positive and negative particles. There are
\[
  \binom{N}{p}^2p!=\frac{(N!)^2}{p!\,(N-p)!^2}
\]
such matchings with $|\mc M|=p$.
  

  We partition the configurations according to this matching and \edgreen{write $u_j$ for the relative position of the two charges of its $j$-th dipole, so that $|u_j|$ is the dipole length.} On each such domain, packing gives $\sum_{j=1}^p|u_j|^2\le CN$\edgreen{: the two endpoints of the $j$-th pair are mutual nearest neighbors, so the discs $B(z_i,|u_j|/2)$ centered at the $2p$ endpoints are disjoint. They lie in $[-\sqrt N,2\sqrt N]^2$, since $|u_j|\le\sqrt{2N}$}. We may therefore insert $e^{-\sum_j|u_j|^2/\ell}$ at cost $e^{CN/\ell}$. We then integrate one endpoint of each pair and the $2N-2p$ remaining particles over $\Lambda$, giving $N^{2N-p}$, and enlarge each \edgreen{integral over a dipole} to $\R^2$. Summing over the matchings yields
  \begin{equation*}
    \K_{N,2}^\lambda\le e^{CN/\ell}\sum_{p=0}^N
    \frac{(N!)^2}{p!(N-p)!^2}N^{2N-p}{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}}^p
    \le N!N^N{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}}^N e^{CN/\ell+2N/\sqrt{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}}}.
  \end{equation*}
  Indeed, setting $d=N-p$ and using $N!/(N-d)!\le N^d$, the sum divided by $N!N^N{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}}^N$ is at most
  \[
    \sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{(N/\sqrt{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}})^{2d}}{(d!)^2}
    \le\left(\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\frac{(N/\sqrt{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}})^d}{d!}\right)^2
    =e^{2N/\sqrt{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}}}.
  \]

  For the lower bound, \edgreen{let} $t=\ell^{-1/2}$. Restrict positive particles to boundary distance at least $t$ and mutual distances at least $4t$, \edgreen{and dipole lengths to $2\lambda\le|u|\le t^2$.} At each placement of a positive particle, the boundary strip excludes area at most $C\sqrt N\,t$, and the previous particles exclude at most $CNt^2$. For $\lambda$ small enough, successive placement therefore gives volume at least
  \[
    N^N e^{-C(\sqrt N\,t+Nt^2)}\ge N^Ne^{-CNt}.
  \]
  Particles in different pairs are farther apart than the partners within each pair. Thus all partners are mutual nearest neighbors, and the $N!$ assignments of negative labels give disjoint configuration sets. Each pair contributes $2\pi(\ell-\log\ell-\log2)$. \edgreen{For the upper bound, \eqref{eq:critical-B} gives $\log \edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}\le\log(2\pi\ell)+C(1+\log\ell)/\ell$ and $\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}\ge\pi\ell$ for $\lambda$ small, so $e^{2N/\sqrt{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}}}\le e^{CN\ell^{-1/2}}$. Since $\log\ell/\ell=O(\ell^{-1/2})$ and $\mc Z_2=2\pi$, the two bounds and Stirling's formula prove the critical expansion, with error $O(N\ell^{-1/2})$.}}

  \edblue{We now assume $\beta>2$. \edgreen{Recall from \eqref{3.20b} that $\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}=\int_{\R^2}e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u)}\dd u$.} The same matching bound, without the Gaussian factor, gives
  \[
    \K_{N,\beta}^\lambda
    \le\sum_{p=0}^N\frac{(N!)^2}{p!(N-p)!^2}N^{2N-p}(\edgreen{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}})^p
    \le N!N^N(\edgreen{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}})^N e^{2N/\sqrt{\edgreen{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}}}}.
  \]
  For the lower bound, take $t=\lambda^{(\beta-2)/\beta}$. Impose the same restrictions on the positive particles, \edgreen{and allow dipole lengths $|u|\le t$.} The preceding placement estimate gives volume at least
  \[
    N^N e^{-C(Nt^2+\sqrt N\,t)}\ge N^N e^{-CNt^2-C}.
  \]
  The last inequality uses $\sqrt N\,t\le Nt^2+1/4$. Points in different pairs are at distance at least $2t$, so the partners are again mutual nearest neighbors and the label assignments are disjoint. For $\lambda$ small enough, $t\ge2\lambda$, and the omitted radial tail is $2\pi t^{2-\beta}/(\beta-2)$. Hence each pair contributes
  \[
    \int_{|u|\le t}e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u)}\dd u
    \ge \edgreen{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}}\left(1-C_\beta(\lambda/t)^{\beta-2}\right).
  \]
  Multiplying these bounds over the pairs and summing over the label assignments gives
  \[
    \K_{N,\beta}^\lambda\ge N!N^N(\edgreen{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}})^N
    \exp\left[-C_\beta N\bigl(t^2+(\lambda/t)^{\beta-2}\bigr)-C\right].
  \]
  Here $t^2=(\lambda/t)^{\beta-2}=\lambda^{2(\beta-2)/\beta}$. Since
  \[
    \frac{2(\beta-2)}{\beta}\ge\min\{(\beta-2)/2,1\},
    \qquad (\edgreen{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}})^{-1/2}=O_\beta(\lambda^{(\beta-2)/2}),
  \]
  the errors in both bounds are $O_\beta(N\bar\omega_\lambda+1)$. Stirling's formula concludes the proof.}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Upper bound on the \texorpdfstring{\edblue{free energy}}{free energy} of $p$ points for a nearest-neighbor model}
We now study a new nearest-neighbor model which will be useful for evaluating the contributions of the dipole-dipole interaction or quadrupole errors that appear in \eqref{minoF02 bis}. We consider an integral over $p$ variables $z_1,\ldots,z_p$ living on $\Lambda^{p}$, where  the nearest  neighbor interaction energy is counted only for a small \edblue{subset} of $k$ points $z_1,\ldots,z_k$.
\edblue{The estimates track the combinatorial cost of placing both endpoints of a $2$-cycle in the marked subset. This cost is useful when $k/p$ is small.}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:auxk}

  \edblue{Let $\beta\ge0$, $0<\lambda\le e^{-1}$ and $0\le k\le p\le N$ be such that $k,p$ are integers and $p\ge\max(2,N/2)$.} Let us consider the energy over $\Lambda^{p}$ defined by
  \begin{equation} \label{defFaux}
    F_k(z_1, \dots, z_p) := \hal \sum_{i=1}^{k}   \log   \rr_1(z_i),
  \end{equation}
  where $\rr_1$ is defined as in \eqref{nn2}.
  Let $\delta=0$ when $\beta\neq 4$ and $\delta\in \{0,1\}$ when $\beta=4$. Let $\delta'=0$ when $\beta\neq 0$ and $\delta'=1$ when $\beta=0$. Then, we have
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{defZaux}
    \log \int_{\Lambda^p} \exp(-\beta F_k(Z_p))\prod_{i=1}^k \Bigl(1+\log \frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\lambda}\Bigr)^{\delta}\prod_{i=1}^k \edblue{\left(1+|\log\rr_1(z_i)|\right)^{\delta'}} \dd z_1\ldots \dd z_p \\ \leq p \log N+C_\beta k+\frac{k}{2}\gamma_{\beta,k} ,
  \end{multline}}
  where
  \edblue{\begin{equation}\label{def:gbetak}
    \gamma_{\beta,k}= \begin{cases}
      0 & \text{if } \beta=0 \text{ and }\delta'=1\\
      0 & \text{if } \ \beta \in (0,2)
      \\ \log\Bigl(|\log \lambda| \frac{k}{N} \vee 1\Bigr)& \text{if } \ \beta =2
      \\ \log\Bigl(\lambda^{2-\beta}\frac{k}{N}\vee 1\Bigr) & \text{if } \ \beta\in (2,4)
      \\ \log\Bigl(\lambda^{-2}\frac{k}{N}\vee |\log\lambda|^{\edblue{2(1+\delta)}}\Bigr)& \text{if $\beta=4$ }
      \\ \log\Bigl(\lambda^{2-\beta}\frac{k}{N}\vee \lambda^{4-\beta}\Bigr) & \text{if } \ \beta>4.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}}
  \edblue{Every occurrence of $\gamma_{4,k}$ uses the current value of $\delta$.}
\end{prop}

\medskip

\begin{proof}
  \edblue{For $k=0$, the integral equals $N^p$. We assume $k\ge1$ below.}
  For brevity, we let $r(z_i)= \frac 14 \min_{j\neq i} |z_i-z_j|$ and note that $\rr_1(z_i) = r(z_i) \edblue{\vee} \lambda$ by \eqref{nn2}.

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 1: counting graphs.}
  We define the directed graph $\gamma_p':=(V,E)$ where the set of vertices $V$ is given by $\{1,\ldots,p\}$ and there is an edge $\vec{ij}\in E$ when $i\in \{1,\ldots,k\}$ and $\edblue{\phi_1(i)=j}$. The graph $\gamma_p'$ is a subgraph of the nearest-neighbor graph $\gamma_p$. We claim that the nontrivial connected components (i.e.,~not reduced to a point) of $\gamma_p'$ are as follows: either they contain a $2$-cycle within $\{1,\ldots, k\}$ and no point in $ V\setminus \{1, \ldots, k\}$,
  or they do not and they contain a unique point in $V\setminus \{1, \ldots, k\}$. In the latter case  the connected component is a tree and  its root is in $V\setminus \{1,\ldots,k\}$.

  Let us prove the above claim. We can observe that $V\setminus \{1,\ldots,k\}$ is exactly the set of vertices of $\gamma_p'$ with no outgoing edge. Let $U$ be a nontrivial connected component of $\gamma_p'$. Since $U$ is contained in a connected component of $\gamma_p$, in view of the structure of $\gamma_p$,  either it contains a $2$-cycle with trees attached to each vertex of the $2$-cycle or it is a tree. In the first case, there is no vertex with $0$ out-degree and therefore no vertex in $V\setminus \{1,\ldots,k\}$. In the second case, the root of $U$ is the only vertex of $U$ with $0$ out-degree and it is therefore the only vertex in $V\setminus \{1,\ldots,k\}$.

  \begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{twotypes.pdf}
    \caption{Nearest-neighbor graph and restricted nearest-neighbor graph}
    \label{fig:twotypes}
  \end{figure}
  Figure \ref{fig:twotypes} shows the points from $\{1, \ldots, k\}$ in black and the points from $V \setminus \{1, \ldots, k\}$ in blue. The solid lines represent the connected components of $\gamma_{p}'$, and the dashed lines represent the edges in $\gamma_p$ that are not part of $\gamma_p'$.

  We denote by $D(k_0,k_1)$ the set of such graphs with $k_0$ $2$-cycles and $k_1$ points in $V\setminus \{1,\ldots,k\}$ that do not form a trivial connected component. \edblue{The number of nontrivial connected components is $k_0+k_1$. Each cycle uses two marked vertices and each rooted tree uses at least one, so $2k_0+k_1\le k$. The measurable cells $\Lambda^p\cap\{\gamma_p'=\gamma\}$ partition $\Lambda^p$ outside the excluded null set.}

  Let us enumerate the  graphs $\gamma$ in $D(k_0,k_1)$.  Let $U_1,\ldots,U_{k_0+k_1}$ be the nontrivial  connected components of such a $\gamma$.

  $\bullet $ Since connected components are \edblue{unordered}, we need to divide by $(k_0+k_1)!$.

  $\bullet$ Among these connected components we choose $k_0$ of them to be those that have  a $2$-cycle. There are $\binom{k_0+k_1}{k_0}$ choices.

  $\bullet$ For these $k_0$ connected components, we choose the
  $2$-cycles. There are
  \begin{equation*}
    \frac{k!}{(k-2k_0)!2^{k_0}}
  \end{equation*}
  choices, corresponding to ordered collections of $k_0$ disjoint unordered pairs chosen from $k$ points.

  $\bullet$ On the $k_1$ trees, we need to fix the label of the root of the tree, which belongs to $\{k+1,\ldots,p\}$. There are at most $p^{k_1}$ choices.

  $\bullet$ To each of the remaining $k-2k_0$ points not in a $2$-cycle we need to assign a nearest-neighbor. Since there are $k_1$ points of $V\setminus \{1,\ldots,k\}$ in the graph, there are at most $k+k_1$ choices. Therefore, the total number of choices for these points is bounded by $(k+k_1)^{k-2k_0}\leq k^{k-2k_0}C^k$.

  Combining these and using Stirling's formula, we obtain the upper bound
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:Dpk}
    \log |D(k_0,k_1)|\le \log \frac{ k! p^{k_1} k^{k-2k_0} C^k (k_0+k_1)!}{(k-2k_0)! 2^{k_0} (k_0+k_1)! k_0! k_1!}
    \\ \leq Ck -k_0\log k_0-k_1\log k_1 +k\log k-(k-2k_0)\log (k-2k_0)+ (k-2k_0)\log k+k_1\log p.
  \end{multline}

  \edblue{\noindent
  {\bf Step 2: integration.}
  Fix $\gamma\in D(k_0,k_1)$ and let $m=k+k_1$ be the number of vertices in its nontrivial connected components. We write
  \[
    I(\gamma):=\int_{\Lambda^p\cap\{\gamma_p'=\gamma\}}e^{-\beta F_k(Z_p)}
    \prod_{i=1}^k\left[\left(1+\log\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\lambda}\right)^\delta
    \left(1+|\log\rr_1(z_i)|\right)^{\delta'}\right]\dd Z_p.
  \]
  We first integrate the $p-m$ vertices in trivial components. Conditional on the other coordinates, the distances $r(z_i)=|z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}|/4$, $i\le k$, are fixed, and each remaining vertex avoids all the balls $B(z_i,4r(z_i))$. The balls $B(z_i,2r(z_i))$, $i\le k$, are disjoint. Moreover, since $r(z_i)\le\sqrt{2N}/4$, each intersection with $\Lambda$ contains a quarter of a disc of radius $\sqrt2r(z_i)$. The available volume is therefore at most $N-c\sum_{i=1}^k r(z_i)^2$.

  If $k\le p/4$, then $p-m\ge p-2k\ge N/4$, so this integration contributes at most
  \[
    N^{p-m}\exp\left(-c\sum_{i=1}^k r(z_i)^2\right).
  \]
  If $k>p/4$, packing gives $\sum_{i=1}^k r(z_i)^2\le CN$. We insert the same exponential at cost $e^{CN}\le e^{Ck}$, and integrate the trivial components in volume $N^{p-m}$.

  In each nontrivial component, we retain the root or the larger-indexed vertex of its $2$-cycle, and \edgreen{replace every other coordinate by its position relative to its parent.} \edgreen{As for \eqref{defphi}, this is an invertible map with determinant $1$.} This leaves $k_0+k_1$ reference points, \edgreen{$k_0$ cycle variables and $k-2k_0$ tree-edge variables.} We integrate the reference points in $\Lambda$ and extend each \edgreen{integral over a variable $u_j$} to $\R^2$.

  For $\beta>0$, polar coordinates, followed by $r=|u|/4$, give the annular integrals
  \[
    \int_\lambda^1 r^{1-\beta}\left(1+\log\frac r\lambda\right)^{2\delta}\dd r,
    \qquad
    \int_\lambda^1 r^{1-\beta/2}\left(1+\log\frac r\lambda\right)^\delta\dd r.
  \]
  The cores contribute $O_\beta(\lambda^{2-\beta})$ and $O_\beta(\lambda^{2-\beta/2})$. The Gaussian tails contribute $O_\beta(\ell^{2\delta})$ and $O_\beta(\ell^\delta)$. Evaluating the annular integrals gives
  \[
    I(\gamma)\le C_\beta^k N^{p-k+k_0}
    \exp\left(k\psi_\beta(k_0/k)\right),
  \]
  where
  \[
    \psi_\beta(x):=\begin{cases}
      0,&0\le\beta<2,\\
      x\log\ell,&\beta=2,\\
      (2-\beta)x\log\lambda,&2<\beta<4,\\
      -2x\log\lambda+(1+\delta)(1-2x)\log\ell,&\beta=4,\\
      (2-\beta)x\log\lambda+(2-\beta/2)(1-2x)\log\lambda,&\beta>4.
    \end{cases}
  \]
  In particular, \edgreen{at $\beta=4$ each tree edge contributes} $C\ell^{1+\delta}$, \edgreen{whereas each cycle contributes} $C\lambda^{-2}$. The absolute logarithms at $\beta=0$ are integrable uniformly in $\lambda$ against the Gaussian factor.

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 3: optimization.}
  The count in Step 1 gives
  \[
    |D(k_0,k_1)|\le\frac{C^k k^k p^{k_1}}{k_0!k_1!}.
  \]
  Using $n!\ge(n/e)^n$, $p\le N$ and $0\log0=0$, we find
  \begin{align*}
    \log|D(k_0,k_1)|+\log I(\gamma)
    &\le p\log N+C_\beta k
    -(k-k_0-k_1)\log(N/k)+k\psi_\beta(k_0/k)\\
    &\le p\log N+C_\beta k
    +k_0\log(k/N)+k\psi_\beta(k_0/k),
  \end{align*}
  where the last inequality uses $2k_0+k_1\le k$. Summing over $k_0,k_1$ costs at most $2\log(k+1)\le Ck$. Hence the logarithm of the integral in the statement is bounded by
  \[
    p\log N+C_\beta k+
    k\max_{0\le x\le1/2}\left\{x\log(k/N)+\psi_\beta(x)\right\}.
  \]
  The function being maximized is affine. Its maximum is attained at $0$ or $1/2$ and equals $\gamma_{\beta,k}/2$, which proves the result.}
\end{proof}

We now prove a variant of Proposition \ref{prop:auxk} allowing two species of points appearing with distinct exponents in the integral.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:two species}Let $\beta >4$. \edblue{Let $0<\lambda\le e^{-1}$ and let $k,k',p$ be nonnegative integers with $k+k'\le p\le N$ and $p\ge\max(2,N/2)$.} Consider
  \begin{equation*}
    F_{k,k'}(z_1,\ldots,z_p):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=k+1}^{k+k'} \log \rr_1(z_i)
  \end{equation*}
  and recall  the definition of $F_k$ from \eqref{defFaux}. Then, for $\delta\in \{0,1\}$, we have
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:two species}
    \log \int_{\Lambda^p}\exp(-4F_k(Z_p)-\beta F_{k,k'}(Z_p))\prod_{i=1}^k \edblue{\left(1+\log\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\lambda}\right)^\delta} \dd z_1\ldots \dd z_p \\ \leq p\log N+C_\beta(k+k')+\frac{k}{2}\gamma_{4,k}+\frac{k'}{2}\gamma_{\beta,k'},
  \end{multline}
  where $\gamma_{\beta,k}$ is as in \eqref{def:gbetak}, \edblue{with the current $\delta$ in $\gamma_{4,k}$}.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  \edblue{If one marked group is empty, the result follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk}. We assume $k,k'>0$ and \edgreen{set} $t=k+k'$. Let $I$ denote the integral in the statement.

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 1: counting graphs.}
  We use $\gamma_p'$ with marked vertices $\{1,\ldots,t\}$. Let $k_{0,1},k_{0,2},k_{0,3}$ count cycles in the first group, in the second group, and between the groups, respectively. \edgreen{Set} $k_0=k_{0,1}+k_{0,2}+k_{0,3}$, and let $k_1$ count the nontrivial tree components. Then
  \[
    2k_{0,1}+k_{0,3}\le k,\qquad
    2k_{0,2}+k_{0,3}\le k',\qquad
    2k_0+k_1\le t.
  \]
  The first two inequalities count the vertices in each group. The third counts two vertices per cycle and at least one marked vertex per rooted tree.
  Disregarding the two species, Step 1 of Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk} bounds the number of graphs with these counts by
  \[
    \frac{C^t t^t p^{k_1}}{k_0!k_1!}.
  \]

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 2: integration.}
  We repeat the Gaussian integration from Step 2 of Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk}, with $t$ marked vertices. A cycle within the first group contributes $C\lambda^{-2}$, and one within the second contributes $C_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}$. For a mixed cycle, dropping the Gaussian and scaling $r=\lambda s$ bounds the radial integral by
  \[
    C\lambda^{-\beta/2}\left(1+\int_1^\infty s^{-1-\beta/2}(1+\log s)^\delta\dd s\right)
    \le C_\beta\lambda^{-\beta/2}.
  \]
  \edgreen{Each tree edge from the first group contributes} $C\ell^{1+\delta}$, and one from the second contributes $C_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta/2}$. Thus, for a fixed graph $\gamma$, its integral is at most
  \begin{multline*}
    C_\beta^tN^{p-t+k_0}
    \lambda^{-2k_{0,1}+(2-\beta)k_{0,2}-(\beta/2)k_{0,3}}
    \ell^{(1+\delta)(k-2k_{0,1}-k_{0,3})}
    \lambda^{(2-\beta/2)(k'-2k_{0,2}-k_{0,3})}\\
    =C_\beta^tN^{p-t+k_0}\lambda^{-2k_0+(2-\beta/2)k'}
    \ell^{(1+\delta)(k-2k_{0,1}-k_{0,3})}.
  \end{multline*}

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 3: optimization.}
  Combining the preceding estimates as in Step 3 of Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk}, and summing over the counts, gives
  \begin{multline*}
    \log I\le p\log N+C_\beta t
    +k(1+\delta)\log\ell+(2-\beta/2)k'\log\lambda\\
    +\max\left\{k_{0,1}A+k_{0,2}B+\frac{k_{0,3}}2(A+B)\right\},
  \end{multline*}
  where the maximum is over the counts above and
  \[
    A:=\log\frac{\lambda^{-2}t}{N}-2(1+\delta)\log\ell,
    \qquad B:=\log\frac{\lambda^{-2}t}{N}.
  \]
  Since $k_{0,1}+k_{0,3}/2\le k/2$ and $k_{0,2}+k_{0,3}/2\le k'/2$, the maximum is at most
  \[
    \frac{k}{2}(A\vee0)+\frac{k'}{2}(B\vee0).
  \]
  Consequently,
  \begin{equation*}
    \log I\le p\log N+C_\beta t
    +\frac{k}{2}\log\left(\frac{\lambda^{-2}t}{N}\vee\ell^{2(1+\delta)}\right)
    +\frac{k'}{2}\log\left(\frac{\lambda^{2-\beta}t}{N}\vee\lambda^{4-\beta}\right).
  \end{equation*}
  Replacing $t$ by $k$ and $k'$ in the respective logarithms costs at most
  \[
    \frac{k}{2}\log(t/k)+\frac{k'}{2}\log(t/k')\le\frac{t}{2}\log2.
  \]
  This proves \eqref{eq:two species}.}
\end{proof}

\edblue{An isolated $2$-cycle $\{i,j\}$ is twice isolated if $\phi_2(i),\phi_2(j)\in I^\pair$.}

\edblue{We now estimate the quadratic errors when all components are isolated pairs. The graph count \eqref{eq:logD}, proved below, gives the entropy and $C_0(p-p_0)$ terms in $f$.}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:isolated}
  \edblue{Let $\beta\ge2$, $p\in\{2,\ldots,N\}$ and $0<\lambda\le e^{-1}$.}
  Let $\gamma_{2p}(Z_{2p})$ be the nearest-neighbor graph of $Z_{2p}$ as defined in Section \ref{sec2}. \edgreen{Let $G_p$ be the set of digraphs on $\{1,\ldots,2p\}$ made of $p$ $2$-cycles. For $\gamma\in G_p$ and a set $S$ of $2$-cycles of $\gamma$, let $I_S$ be the set of vertices of the $2$-cycles in $S$.}

  Let $\edblue{C,}C_0>0$. For all $\edgreen{\gamma\in G_p}$ \edgreen{and every such $S$}, set
  \edblue{\begin{equation*}
    I(\gamma\edgreen{,S}):=\int_{\Omega_\gamma} \exp\Bigg(-\beta \Bigl(\F_\lambda^{\nn}(Z_{2p}) - C\sum_{i\notin \edgreen{I_S}} \Bigl(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\Bigr)^2 + \sum_{i\in \edgreen{I_S}} \Big(\log \frac{\rr_2(z_i)\wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)} ) }{\rr_1(z_i)} -C \Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigg) \dd Z_{2p}
  \end{equation*}}
  and for each $p_0\in \{0,\ldots,p\}$, set
  \begin{equation*}
    f(p_0):=p\log p-p_0\log p_0-(p-p_0)\log(p-p_0)+C_0(p-p_0)  +\edgreen{\max_{\gamma\in G_p,\ |S|=p-p_0}}\log I(\gamma\edgreen{,S}).
  \end{equation*}
  \edblue{In this proposition, all constants, including those in $O(\cdot)$, may depend on $\beta,C,C_0$.}

  Assume that $p\geq \frac{N}{2}$. For any $\beta\geq 2$, we have
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:n neutral}
    \max_{p_0\in \{0,\ldots,p\}} f(p_0)
    \leq
    p\Bigl( \log N +(2-\beta) (\log  \lambda) \indic_{\beta>2}+(\log |\log \lambda|)\indic_{\beta=2}+ \log \mc{Z}_\beta \\+ C_\beta(|\log\lambda|^{-1}\indic_{\beta=2}+ \lambda^{\beta-2}\indic_{2<\beta<4}+\lambda^2|\log\lambda|^2\indic_{\beta=4}+(\lambda^2|\log\lambda|)\indic_{\beta>4})\Bigr)\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)}.
  \end{multline}}
  In addition, there exists $c_\beta>0$ such that for every $p_0\le p$,
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:n neutral conv}
    f(p_0)-p\Bigl( \log N +(2-\beta) (\log  \lambda) \indic_{\beta>2}+(\log |\log \lambda|)\indic_{\beta=2}+ \log \mc{Z}_\beta\Bigr) \leq -c_\beta p \Bigl(\frac{p-p_0}{p}\Bigr)^2\\+O\Bigl(p\Bigl(|\log\lambda|^{-1}\indic_{\beta=2}+ \lambda^{\beta-2}\indic_{2<\beta<4}+\edblue{\lambda^2|\log\lambda|^2}\indic_{\beta=4}+(\lambda^2|\log\lambda|)\indic_{\beta>4}\Bigr)\Bigr)\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)}.
  \end{multline}}

  Finally, for any $\beta\geq 2$, there exists $C_\beta>0$ such that for every $\edblue{2\le}p<\frac{N}{2}$,
  \edblue{\begin{equation}\label{eq:crude}
    \max_{p_0\in \{0,\ldots,p\}}f(p_0)
    \leq
    p\Bigl( \log N +(2-\beta) (\log  \lambda )\indic_{\beta>2}+(\log |\log \lambda|)\indic_{\beta=2}\Bigr)+C_\beta N.
  \end{equation}}
\end{prop}
\medskip

\begin{proof}
  \edblue{\edgreen{We integrate first over the relative positions within the pairs and then over one representative of each pair.}

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  {\bf Step 1: comparison of the radii.}
  Fix $\edgreen{\gamma\in G_p}$ \edgreen{and $S$ with $|S|=p-p_0$}. \edgreen{We retain one endpoint $w_j$ of each pair and write the other endpoint as $w_j+u_j$.} Let
  \[
    \rho_j:=\left(\frac14\min_{l\ne j}|w_j-w_l|\right)\vee\lambda,
    \qquad a_j:=(|u_j|/4)\vee\lambda.
  \]
  On $\{\gamma_{2p}=\gamma\}$, we have $|u_j|\le4\rho_j$ and $a_j\le\rho_j$.
  For either endpoint $z$ of the pair, let $v$ be its second nearest neighbor and let $w_l$ be the retained endpoint of the pair containing $v$. The distance from $v$ to $w_l$ is at most $|z-v|$, and the distance from $z$ to $w_j$ is also at most $|z-v|$. Hence $\rho_j\le3\rr_2(z)$, which gives\edgreen{, since $\rr_1(z)=a_j$,}
  \begin{equation}\label{mainclaimr1r2}
    \frac{\rr_1(z)}{\rr_2(z)}\le\edgreen{3}\frac{a_j}{\rho_j}.
  \end{equation}
  The change of variables $Z_{2p}\mapsto(U_p,W_p)$ has Jacobian $1$. \edgreen{The factor corresponding to a pair not in $S$} is bounded above in the integrand by
  $e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u_j)+C_\beta(a_j/\rho_j)^2}$.
  \edgreen{The factor corresponding to a pair in $S$} is bounded above by
  $C_\beta e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u_j)}(a_j/\rho_j)^{2\beta}$.
  \edgreen{We enlarge the domain of $u_j$ to $|u_j|\le4\rho_j$.}

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  {\bf \edgreen{Step 2: integration over the pairs.}}
  For $\rho\ge\lambda$, the inequality
  $e^{C_\beta s^2}\le1+C_\beta' s^2$ for $0\le s\le1$, followed by radial integration at $2\lambda$ and $4\rho$, gives
  \begin{equation}\label{align1}
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u)}
    e^{C_\beta(((|u|/4)\vee\lambda)/\rho)^2}\dd u
    \le\mc Z_\beta\left(\lambda^{2-\beta}\indic_{\beta>2}
    +\ell\,\indic_{\beta=2}\right)\left(1+C_\beta'\varphi_\beta(\rho)\right),
  \end{equation}
  where $C_\beta'>0$ depends on the fixed coefficient in the exponential and, for $x\ge\lambda$,
  \begin{equation}\label{defvarphi}
    \varphi_\beta(x):=\begin{cases}
      (1+|\log x|)/\ell,&\beta=2,\\
      (\lambda/x)^{\beta-2},&2<\beta<4,\\
      (\lambda/x)^2(1+\log(x/\lambda)),&\beta=4,\\
      (\lambda/x)^2,&\beta>4.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  For $\beta=2$, scaling on $|u|<2\lambda$ and $\g_\lambda(u)=-\log|u|$ outside give
  \[
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}\dd u
    =2\pi(\ell+\log\rho)+O(1)
  \]
  \edgreen{(see \eqref{eq:critical-disc}). Here no Gaussian factor is needed, since the pair is confined to the disc $|u|\le4\rho$.}
  The quadratic correction is bounded by
  \[
    C_\beta\rho^{-2}
    \left(\lambda^{4-\beta}+\int_{2\lambda}^{4\rho}r^{3-\beta}\dd r\right).
  \]
  \edgreen{For a pair in $S$}, the radial decomposition gives the bound
  \[
    C_\beta\rho^{-2\beta}\left(\lambda^{\beta+2}
    +\int_{2\lambda}^{4\rho}r^{\beta+1}\dd r\right)
    \le C_\beta\rho^{2-\beta}.
  \]
  Hence
  \begin{equation}\label{align2}
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u)}
    \left(\frac{(|u|/4)\vee\lambda}{\rho}\right)^{2\beta}\dd u
    \le C_\beta\rho^{2-\beta}.
  \end{equation}
  For $A>0$ and $S\subset\{1,\ldots,p\}$, define
  \begin{multline}\label{pred3}
    \mathcal J_{A,S}:=
    \mc Z_\beta^p\left(\lambda^{2-\beta}\indic_{\beta>2}
    +\ell\,\indic_{\beta=2}\right)^p\\
    \times\int_{\Lambda^p}
    \prod_{j\notin S}\left(1+A\varphi_\beta(\rho_j)\right)
    \prod_{j\in S}
    A\left(\ell^{-1}\indic_{\beta=2}
    +(\lambda/\rho_j)^{\beta-2}\indic_{\beta>2}\right)\dd W_p.
  \end{multline}
  \edgreen{We} obtain $I(\gamma\edgreen{,S})\le\mathcal J_{A,S}$ for a fixed $A$ depending only on $\beta,C$.

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  {\bf Step 3: integration of the representatives.}
  We prove that \eqref{eq:n neutral}, \eqref{eq:n neutral conv} and \eqref{eq:crude} also hold with $f(p_0)$ replaced by
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:representative-bound}
    p\log p-p_0\log p_0-q\log q+C_0q+\log\mathcal J_{A,S},
    \qquad q=|S|=p-p_0.
  \end{equation}
  The constants depend only on $\beta,A,C_0$, uniformly in $S$. Assume first $p\ge N/2$. We expand the first product in \eqref{pred3}, with $k$ selected factors, and use Propositions~\ref{prop:auxk} and~\ref{prop:two species}. The sum over $0\le k\le p_0$ costs at most $\log(p+1)$ beyond its largest term.
  Set $x_0=q/p$, $x_1=k/p$ and $t=x_0+x_1\le1$. We use
  \[
    \log\binom{p_0}{k}\le k\log(p/k)+k,\qquad
    -(1-x_0)\log(1-x_0)\le x_0,
  \]
  and
  \[
    -x_0\log x_0-x_1\log x_1\le t\log(2/t).
  \]
  The following expressions bound each term, divided by $p$, after subtracting
  \[
    \log N+(2-\beta)\log\lambda\,\indic_{\beta>2}
    +\log\ell\,\indic_{\beta=2}+\log\mc Z_\beta
  \]
  and adding the entropy and $C_0q$ from $f$.

  If $\beta=2$, Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk} with $\beta=0$ gives the bound
  \[
    t\left(-\log t-\log\ell+C_\beta\right).
  \]
  If $2<\beta<4$, \edgreen{set} $b=\beta-2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk} with parameter $2b$ and $k+q$ selected points gives
  \[
    t\left(b\log\lambda-\log t+C_\beta\right)
    +\frac{t}{2}\begin{cases}
      0,&0<b<1,\\
      \log(\ell t\vee1),&b=1,\\
      \log(\lambda^{2-2b}t\vee1),&1<b<2.
    \end{cases}
  \]
  Here we used $p/N\le1$ in \eqref{def:gbetak}.
  If $\beta=4$, we bound the factors \edgreen{indexed by $S$} by adding
  $1+\log(\rho_j/\lambda)\ge1$. Proposition~\ref{prop:auxk} with parameter $4$, $\delta=1$ and $k+q$ selected points then gives
  \[
    t\left(2\log\lambda-\log t+C_\beta\right)
    +\frac{t}{2}\log(\lambda^{-2}t\vee\ell^4).
  \]
  Finally, if $\beta>4$, \edgreen{set} $b=\beta-2>2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:two species} with parameter $2b$ and $\delta=0$ gives
  \begin{multline*}
    x_0\left(b\log\lambda-\log x_0+C_\beta\right)
    +\frac{x_0}{2}\log(\lambda^{2-2b}x_0\vee\lambda^{4-2b})\\
    +x_1\left(2\log\lambda-\log x_1+C_\beta\right)
    +\frac{x_1}{2}\log(\lambda^{-2}x_1\vee\ell^2).
  \end{multline*}
  Empty selected groups contribute no term.

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  {\bf Step 4: optimization.}
  For $a\in\mathbb R$ and \edgreen{$\eta>0$},
  \[
    \edgreen{\sup_{0<s\le1}s\left(a+\eta\log(1/s)\right)\le \eta e^{a/\eta-1}.}
  \]
  We apply this inequality to each branch of the maxima above.
  For $\beta=2$, it gives $C_\beta/\ell$.
  For $2<\beta<4$, the branch with no extra logarithm gives
  $C_\beta\lambda^{\beta-2}$. The other branch gives
  $C_\beta\lambda^2\ell$ at $\beta=3$ and
  $C_\beta\lambda^2$ for $3<\beta<4$, both bounded by
  $C_\beta\lambda^{\beta-2}$.
  For $\beta=4$, the two branches give
  $C_\beta\lambda^2$ and $C_\beta\lambda^2\ell^2$.
  For $\beta>4$, the $x_0$ terms give $C_\beta\lambda^2$, and the $x_1$ terms give $C_\beta\lambda^2\ell$.
  This proves \eqref{eq:n neutral}.
  Adding $x_0^2\le t$ to the expressions in Step 3 only changes $C_\beta$.
  The same optimization therefore proves \eqref{eq:n neutral conv}.

  For $p<N/2$, each factor in \eqref{pred3} is bounded by $1+A$ when $\beta>2$, and by $(1+2A)e^{\max(\log\rho_j,0)}$ when $\beta=2$. Packing gives $\sum_j\rho_j^2\le CN$. Since
  \[
    \max(\log\rho_j,0)\le\frac{p\rho_j^2}{2N}+\frac12\log(N/p),
    \qquad p\log(N/p)\le N,
  \]
  the representative integral is at most $N^p(1+2A)^p e^{CN}$. The entropy is at most $p\log2$, which proves \eqref{eq:crude} for \eqref{eq:representative-bound} and hence for $f$.}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Main result}
We may now obtain the main upper bound.

\edblue{Let us denote by $D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}$ the set of nearest-neighbor graphs of configurations $Z_{2N}$, marked at all their twice-isolated $2$-cycles, with $K$ connected components, $p$ marked cycles and $\edblue{\widehat p_0}$ neutral marked cycles. A marked graph is the pair consisting of the nearest-neighbor graph and its set of twice-isolated cycles. The condition $\gamma_{2N}=\gamma$ includes these marks, and the measurable cells $\Omega_\gamma:=\Lambda^{2N}\cap\{\gamma_{2N}=\gamma\}$ partition $\Lambda^{2N}$ outside the excluded null set.}

\begin{prop}[Upper bound]\label{prop:upper bound}
  \edblue{Fix $\beta\ge2$. The bounds below hold uniformly in $N\ge2$ for $0<\lambda<\lambda_\beta$, where $\lambda_\beta\in(0,e^{-2}]$ depends only on $\beta$.}
  Let $\omega_\lambda$ be  as in \eqref{eq:defgammal}.
  \edblue{We have}
  \begin{multline}\label{ubz}
    \log \Z\leq 2N\log N+N\Big((2-\beta)(\log \lambda)\indic_{\beta>2}+(\log|\log\lambda|)\indic_{\beta=2}+\log \mc{Z}_{\beta}-1
    +O_\beta(\omega_\lambda)\Big)\\\edblue{+O_\beta(\log(N+1))},
  \end{multline}where  $\mc{Z}_\beta$ is the constant defined in \eqref{def:Cbeta}. Moreover, \edblue{for each $\edgreen{p_0}\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$, let}
  \begin{equation*}
    \edblue{\mc A(\edgreen{p_0}):=\{|I^\dip|=2\edgreen{p_0}\}}.
  \end{equation*}
  There exists $c_\beta>0$ such that
  \edblue{\begin{multline}\label{eq:neutral bound}
    \edblue{\log}\int_{\edblue{\Lambda^{2N}\cap\mc A(\edgreen{p_0})}}e^{-\beta \F_\lambda(X_N,Y_N)}\dd X_N \dd Y_N \leq  2N\log N +N((2-\beta)(\log \lambda)\indic_{\beta>2}+\log|\log \lambda|\indic_{\beta=2})\\
    +N(\log \mc{Z}_\beta-1)-c_\beta N \Bigl(\frac{\edgreen{p_0}-N}{N}\Bigr)^2+O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)}.
  \end{multline}}

  \edblue{Let $D_R$ count the isolated dipoles of length greater than $R$. For $\beta>2$, $R\ge2\lambda$ and $p_0'\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$, let $x_0=p_0'/N$ and $\alpha=\mu_\beta([R/\lambda,+\infty))$. There exists $C_\beta>0$ such that, for $x_0\ge C_\beta\alpha$, we have
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:u bound}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N}\cap\{D_R\ge p_0'\}}e^{-\beta\F_\lambda}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le2N\log N+N\bigl((2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta-1\bigr)\\
    -c_\beta Nx_0\log(x_0/\alpha)+O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)+C_\beta\log(N+1).
  \end{multline}}

  \edblue{For $\beta=2$ and $2\lambda\le R\le1$, we have
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:critical upper}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N}}e^{-2\F_\lambda+N-\frac12|I^{\dip}|+D_R}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le 2N\log N+N\bigl(\log(2\pi|\log\lambda|)-1\bigr)\\
    +CN\left(\omega_\lambda+
    \edgreen{\frac{\log(1/R)}{|\log\lambda|}}\right)+C\log(N+1),
  \end{multline}
  where $C>0$ is independent of $N,\lambda,R$.}
\end{prop}

\medskip

\begin{proof} \edblue{We first assume $\beta>2$.} Again, we split the phase-space according to the nearest neighbor graph $\gamma_{2N}$ of the points.

  \noindent
  {\bf{Step 1: enumeration of graphs.}} Let us enumerate $D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}$, \ed{defined just above the proposition}. Let $\gamma\in D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}$. Consider the ordered connected components of $\gamma$.

  $\bullet$ One must first divide by $K!$ to account for the fact that connected components are \edblue{unordered}.

  $\bullet$ We then select the connected components that are a twice isolated $2$-cycle (\ed{so that there are $p$ of them}), a neutral twice isolated $2$-cycle (\ed{so that there are $\edblue{\widehat p_0}$ of them}). This gives
  \begin{equation*}
    \frac{K!}{\edblue{\widehat p_0}!(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})!(K-p)!}
  \end{equation*}
  possible choices.

  $\bullet$ Labelling the vertices of the $\edblue{\widehat p_0}$ twice isolated neutral $2$-cycles gives
  \begin{equation*}
    \frac{(N!)^2}{((N-\edblue{\widehat p_0})!)^2}=\frac{(2N)!}{2^{2\edblue{\widehat p_0}}(2N-2\edblue{\widehat p_0})!}e^{O(\log N)}
  \end{equation*}
  possible choices (after using Stirling's formula).

  $\bullet$ The number of ways to label the other $2$-cycles is bounded by the number of ways to form $K-\edblue{\widehat p_0}$ pairs with $2N-2\edblue{\widehat p_0}$ points, which is
  \begin{equation*}
    \frac{(2N-2\edblue{\widehat p_0})!}{(2N-2K)!2^{K-\edblue{\widehat p_0}}}.
  \end{equation*}

  $\bullet$ Finally, assigning a nearest-neighbor to each vertex not in a $2$-cycle gives at most $(2N)^{2N-2K}$ possible choices.

  Combining the above we obtain
  \begin{equation*}
    |D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}|\leq \frac{(2N)!(2N)^{2N-2K}}{(2N-2K)!2^{K+\edblue{\widehat p_0}} \edblue{\widehat p_0}!(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})!(K-p)! }e^{O(\log N)}.
  \end{equation*}
  Hence, using Stirling's formula,
  \begin{multline*}
    \log |D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}|\ed{\le} 2N\log (2N)-2N+(2N-2K)\log (2N)-(2N-2K)\log (2N-2K)+2N-2K\\-(K+\edblue{\widehat p_0})\log 2-\edblue{\widehat p_0}\log \edblue{\widehat p_0}+\edblue{\widehat p_0}-(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})\log (p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})+p-\edblue{\widehat p_0}-(K-p)\log(K-p)+K-p+O(\log N)\\
    =2N\log (2N)-(2N-2K)\log \frac{N-K}{N}-(K+\edblue{\widehat p_0})\log 2\\ -\edblue{\widehat p_0}\log \edblue{\widehat p_0}-(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})\log (p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})-(K-p)\log(K-p)-K+O(\log N).
  \end{multline*}

  Let us denote by $m$ the total number of isolated $2$-cycles. \edblue{For each of the $m-p$ isolated pairs that are not twice isolated, choose a vertex whose second nearest neighbor belongs to $(I^\pair)^c$. By Remark~\ref{remark:maximal5}, at most six such pairs map to each exterior point. Hence
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:r1}
    m-p\le6|(I^\pair)^c|=12(N-m).
  \end{equation}
  Each of the $K-m$ nonisolated components contains a tree vertex, so
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:r2}
    K-m\le2(N-K).
  \end{equation}
  Thus $|(I^\pair)^c|=2(N-K)+2(K-m)\le6(N-K)$, which gives $m-p\le M_0(N-K)$ with $M_0=36$, and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:K-p}
    K-p\le(M_0+2)(N-K).
  \end{equation}
  Using this bound in the graph count, we obtain, for a constant $C_0>0$,}
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:logD}
    \log |D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}|\leq  2N\log N\edblue{-} (2N-2K)\log \frac{N-K}{N} -\edblue{\widehat p_0}\log \edblue{\widehat p_0}-(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})\log (p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})\\ -(K-p)\log(K-p)-N+\edblue{O(\log(N+1))}+C_0(N-K)+C_0(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0}).
  \end{multline}

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 2: Inserting the energy lower bound and splitting the integral.}

  For each $\edblue{\widehat p_0}\leq p\leq K\leq N$, let us define
  \begin{equation*}
    I(K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}):=\max_{\gamma\in D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}}\log \int_{\edblue{\Omega_\gamma}}e^{-\beta \F_\lambda(X_N,Y_N)}\dd X_N \dd Y_N.
  \end{equation*}

  To bound $I(K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0})$ from above, we start by inserting the lower bound \eqref{minoF02 bis} into the definition of $I(K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0})$, which gives
  \edblue{\begin{equation}\label{gre1}
  \begin{aligned}
    I(K,p,\widehat p_0)&\leq \max_{\gamma\in D_{2N,K,p,\widehat p_0}}\log \int_{\Lambda^{2N}\cap\{\gamma_{2N}=\gamma\}} \exp\Bigg(-\beta \Bigg(\F_\lambda^{\nn}(X_N, Y_N) \\
    &\qquad+\sum_{\substack{i\in I^{\pair}\backslash I^{\dip}\\ \phi_2(i) \in I^\pair, \phi_2(\phi_1(i)) \in I^\pair}}\( \log
    \frac{\min(\rr_2(z_i), \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)}))}{\rr_1(z_i)}-C\)\\
    &\qquad-C \sum_{\substack{i \in I^{\dip} ,\phi_2(i)\in I^\pair\\ \phi_2(\phi_1(i))\in I^\pair }}
    \( \frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\)^2\Bigg)\Bigg)\dd X_N\dd Y_N+C_\beta(N-K),
  \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}}
  where we recall that $I^\pair$ and $ I^\dip$ are defined in \eqref{defIdip}.

  \edblue{Fix $\gamma\in D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}$. Let $I=I^{\pair}$, with $|I|=2m$, and let $I'$ be the set of vertices of the $p-\edblue{\widehat p_0}$ marked same-sign pairs. For $m\ge2$, define
  \begin{multline}\label{def:F(1)}
    F^{(1)}((z_i)_{i\in I}):=-\frac12\sum_{i\in I}\g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)})
    +\sum_{i\in I'}\left(\log\frac{\rr_2(z_i)\wedge\rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})}{\rr_1(z_i)}-C\right)\\
    -C\sum_{i\in I\setminus I'}\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\right)^2,
  \end{multline}
  where the distances are computed within $I$ and $I'$ remains fixed. Thus the $m-p$ unmarked pairs receive the quadratic correction, irrespective of their signs. On the complement, define
  \begin{equation}\label{def:F(2)}
    F^{(2)}((z_i)_{i\in I^c}):=-\frac12\sum_{i\in I^c}\g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}).
  \end{equation}
  Both $I$ and $I^c$ are unions of whole components, so each vertex retains its nearest neighbor and $\rr_1$. At marked vertices, the second neighbor also lies in $I$, so $\rr_2$ is unchanged there. If $m\le1$, there are no marked pairs, since a second neighbor in $I$ would require another isolated pair. We then use only the nearest-neighbor term in $F^{(1)}$. The additional quadratic terms are nonpositive, so Corollary~\ref{coromino} gives
  \[
    \F_\lambda\ge F^{(1)}+F^{(2)}-C(N-K).
  \]
  Let $\gamma^1$ and $\gamma^2$ be the restrictions of the underlying nearest-neighbor graph to $I$ and $I^c$. Enlarging the integration domain now separates the variables:
  \begin{multline}\label{gred3}
    I(K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0})\le\max_{\gamma\in D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}}\Bigg(
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2m}\cap\{\gamma_{2m}=\gamma^1\}}e^{-\beta F^{(1)}}\prod_{i\in I}\dd z_i\\
    +\log\int_{\Lambda^{2N-2m}\cap\{\gamma_{2N-2m}=\gamma^2\}}e^{-\beta F^{(2)}}\prod_{i\in I^c}\dd z_i+C_\beta(N-K)\Bigg).
  \end{multline}

  For $p\ge N/2$, Proposition~\ref{prop:isolated} applies with $m$ pairs \edgreen{and $S$ the set of the $p-\widehat p_0$ marked pairs, so that} $\edblue{\widehat p_0}+m-p$ \edgreen{pairs} are assigned the quadratic correction. For $q=p-\widehat p_0$, the function $s\mapsto s\log s-(s-q)\log(s-q)$ has derivative $\log(s/(s-q))\ge0$ for $s>q$ and is continuous at $q$. Hence
  \[
    m\log m-(\edblue{\widehat p_0}+m-p)\log(\edblue{\widehat p_0}+m-p)\ge p\log p-\edblue{\widehat p_0}\log \edblue{\widehat p_0},
    \qquad
    \frac{(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})^2}{m}\ge\frac{(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})^2}{N}.
  \]
  Using \eqref{eq:representative-bound} with the constant $C_0$ in \eqref{eq:logD}, we obtain
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:F(1)}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2m}\cap\{\gamma_{2m}=\gamma^1\}}e^{-\beta F^{(1)}}\prod_{i\in I}\dd z_i
    +p\log p-\edblue{\widehat p_0}\log \edblue{\widehat p_0}-(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})\log(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})+C_0(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})\\
    \le m\bigl(\log N+(2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta\bigr)
    +O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)-c_\beta N\left(\frac{p-\edblue{\widehat p_0}}{N}\right)^2+C_\beta\log(N+1).
  \end{multline}
  Here $\omega_\lambda$ is as in \eqref{eq:defgammal}. The graph $\gamma^2$ has $K-m$ components and $2(N-m)$ vertices. For $m=N$, its integral is $1$ and contributes zero to \eqref{gred3}. For $m<N$, Lemma~\ref{lemma:nearest neigh} gives
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:F(2)}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N-2m}\cap\{\gamma_{2N-2m}=\gamma^2\}}e^{-\beta F^{(2)}}\prod_{i\in I^c}\dd z_i
    \le(K-m)\bigl(\log N+(2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta\bigr)\\
    +(2N-2K)\Bigg(\left(1-\frac\beta4\right)\log\frac{N}{N-K}\indic_{\beta<4}
    +\log\left(|\log\lambda|+\log\frac{N}{N-K}\right)\indic_{\beta=4}\\
    +\left(2-\frac\beta2\right)\log\lambda\indic_{\beta>4}\Bigg)+C_\beta(N-K).
  \end{multline}}

  \edblue{Set $\delta=(N-K)/N$ and $\alpha_0=M_0+2$\edgreen{, and recall that $\ell\ge1$}. By \eqref{eq:K-p},
  \[
    -\frac pN\log\frac pN-\frac{K-p}{N}\log\frac{K-p}{N}
    \le\alpha_0\delta\log(1/\delta)+C\delta.
  \]
  Combining \eqref{eq:logD}, \eqref{gred3}, \eqref{eq:F(1)} and \eqref{eq:F(2)}, we obtain, for $p\ge N/2$,
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:2cases p}
    \log|D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}|+I(K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0})
    \le2N\log N+N\bigl((2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta-1\bigr)\\
    +N\chi_\beta(\delta)-c_\beta\frac{(p-\edblue{\widehat p_0})^2}{N}
    +C_\beta N\omega_\lambda+C_\beta\log(N+1),
  \end{multline}
  where $\chi_\beta(0)=0$ and, for $0<\delta\le1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{defchi}
    \chi_\beta(\delta):=\begin{cases}
      \delta\bigl[-(\beta-2)\ell+(\alpha_0+4-\beta/2)\log(1/\delta)+C_\beta\bigr],&2<\beta<4,\\
      \delta\bigl[-2\ell+2\log\ell+(\alpha_0+4)\log(1/\delta)+C_\beta\bigr],&\beta=4,\\
      \delta\bigl[-2\ell+(\alpha_0+2)\log(1/\delta)+C_\beta\bigr],&\beta>4.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  For $\beta=4$, we used $\ell+\log(1/\delta)\le\ell/\delta$. For $p<N/2$, we use \eqref{eq:crude} if $2\le m<N/2$ and \eqref{eq:n neutral} if $m\ge N/2$. For $m\le1$, direct integration gives the same crude bound. This gives \eqref{eq:2cases p} with $C_\beta N$ in place of $C_\beta N\omega_\lambda$, after absorbing the quadratic term into this error.}

  \noindent
  {\bf{Step 3: optimization.}} \edblue{For $A\in\mathbb R$ and $B>0$, we have
  \[
    \sup_{0<\delta\le1}\delta\bigl(A+B\log(1/\delta)\bigr)\le B e^{A/B-1}.
  \]
  Applying this to \eqref{defchi}, we obtain
  \[
    \sup_{0\le\delta\le1}\chi_\beta(\delta)\le C_\beta\begin{cases}
      \lambda^{\frac{2(\beta-2)}{12-\beta+2M_0}},&2<\beta<4,\\
      (\lambda\ell)^{\frac{2}{M_0+6}},&\beta=4,\\
      \lambda^{\frac{2}{M_0+4}},&\beta>4.
    \end{cases}
  \]
  These bounds are $O_\beta(\omega_\lambda)$\edgreen{; up to constants, they are the rates in \eqref{eq:defgammal}}. Applying the same estimate to $\chi_\beta(\delta)+\delta^2$, using $\delta^2\le\delta$, gives $\chi_\beta(\delta)\le-\delta^2+C_\beta\omega_\lambda$. Since
  \[
    1-\frac{\edblue{\widehat p_0}}{N}\le(1+\alpha_0)\delta+\frac{p-\edblue{\widehat p_0}}{N},
  \]
  we deduce from \eqref{eq:2cases p} that, for $p\ge N/2$,
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:quadra x3}
    \log|D_{2N,K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0}}|+I(K,p,\edblue{\widehat p_0})
    \le2N\log N+N\bigl((2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta-1\bigr)\\
    -c_\beta\frac{(N-\edblue{\widehat p_0})^2}{N}+C_\beta N\omega_\lambda+C_\beta\log(N+1).
  \end{multline}
  For $p<N/2$, we have $\delta\ge[2(1+\alpha_0)]^{-1}$. In this range $\chi_\beta(\delta)\to-\infty$ uniformly as $\lambda\to0$, so \eqref{eq:quadra x3} also follows from the crude estimate above.

  Summing over $K,p,\widehat p_0$ costs at most $3\log(N+1)$, proving \eqref{ubz}. On $\mc A(\edgreen{p_0})$, the marked count satisfies
  \[
    \widehat p_0\le \edgreen{p_0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    -c_\beta\frac{(N-\widehat p_0)^2}{N}
    \le-c_\beta\frac{(N-\edgreen{p_0})^2}{N}.
  \]
  Summing \eqref{eq:quadra x3} over the classes compatible with $\mc A(\edgreen{p_0})$ proves \eqref{eq:neutral bound}.} \edblue{For $\beta>2$, dropping the nonnegative quadratic correction from the exponent in \eqref{gre1} gives}
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:alt}
    \edblue{\log}\int_{\edblue{\Lambda^{2N}}} e^{-\beta G(Z_{2N})} \dd Z_{2N}\edblue{\le}2N\log N
    +N\edblue{\bigl((2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc{Z}_\beta-1\bigr)}\\
    +\edblue{O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)}\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)},
  \end{multline}
  where
  \begin{equation*}
    G:=-\hal \sum_{ i=1}^{2N} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} )\edblue{+}  \sum_{\substack{i\in I^{\pair}\backslash I^{\dip}\\ \phi_2(i) \in I^\pair, \phi_2(\phi_1(i)) \in I^\pair}}\( \log
    \frac{\rr_2(z_i) \wedge \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i)})}{\rr_1(z_i)}-C\).
  \end{equation*}

  \medskip

  \noindent
  {\bf{Step 4: control on the size of dipoles.}}
  \edblue{For $\beta>2$, we use the separation \eqref{gred3}. In the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:isolated}, the quadratic correction in each radial integral is bounded. Requiring \edgreen{$|u_j|>R$ for a pair $j\notin S$} therefore bounds its radial factor by
  \[
    C_\beta\int_{|u|\ge R}e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u)}\dd u
    =C_\beta\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}\mu_\beta([R/\lambda,+\infty)).
  \]
  The bound \eqref{align1} without a length restriction is at least $\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}$. Thus each prescribed long pair \edgreen{$j\notin S$} contributes a factor $C_\beta\mu_\beta([R/\lambda,+\infty))$ \edgreen{to} $\mathcal J_{A,S}$ in \eqref{pred3}. The bounds \eqref{eq:representative-bound} therefore apply uniformly to every prescribed set of long pairs. \edgreen{This includes the branch $p<N/2$, since \eqref{eq:n neutral} and \eqref{eq:crude} are proved there for \eqref{eq:representative-bound} itself.} For $m\le1$, direct pair integration gives the same tail factor.

  For $m\ge2$, every isolated neutral pair lies in $I\setminus I'$ and receives the quadratic correction in $F^{(1)}$. On $\{D_R\ge p_0'\}$, at least $p_0'$ such pairs are long. Summing over their choices costs at most $\binom{N}{p_0'}$. The graph sum is otherwise the same as in Steps 2 and 3, so
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:A'}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N}\cap\{D_R\ge p_0'\}}e^{-\beta\F_\lambda}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le2N\log N+N\bigl((2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta-1\bigr)\\
    +\log\binom{N}{p_0'}+p_0'\log\bigl(C_\beta\mu_\beta([R/\lambda,+\infty))\bigr)
    +C_\beta N\omega_\lambda+C_\beta\log(N+1).
  \end{multline}
  For $x_0=p_0'/N$, $\alpha=\mu_\beta([R/\lambda,+\infty))$ and $x_0\ge C_\beta'\alpha$, choosing $C_\beta'$ large enough gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:claim F(1)}
    \log\binom{N}{p_0'}+p_0'\log(C_\beta\alpha)
    \le-Nx_0\log\frac{x_0}{eC_\beta\alpha}
    \le-\frac12Nx_0\log\frac{x_0}{\alpha}.
  \end{equation}
  This proves \eqref{eq:u bound}.}

  \edgreen{\noindent{\bf The case $\beta=2$: strategy.} For $\beta>2$, the argument above isolates the twice-isolated pairs. The reason is that the dipole mass $\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}$ sits at scale $\lambda$, while the quadratic correction of a pair at distance $\rho$ from the other points has relative cost $\varphi_\beta(\rho)$ by \eqref{align1}. This is small only when $\rho\gg\lambda$, and controlling the pairs with small $\rho$ requires Propositions~\ref{prop:auxk}--\ref{prop:isolated} and the counting \eqref{eq:r1}--\eqref{eq:K-p}, which introduces $M_0$. For $\beta=2$, this separation is unnecessary. By Lemma~\ref{lem:critical radial}, a neutral pair confined to $|u|\le4\rho$ has mass $2\pi(\ell+\log\rho)+O(1)$, and the quadratic correction changes this mass by $O(1)$, uniformly in $\rho\ge\lambda$. Compared with $\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}\approx2\pi\ell$, this is a relative error $O(1/\ell)$. We may therefore treat all isolated pairs at once, conditionally on the remaining points. The logarithmic mass is lost in two ways: by a same-sign isolated pair, whose energy correction removes it, and by each of the $N-K$ missing components. With $\delta=(N-K)/N$, this loss is $e^{-\delta N\log\ell}$, while the number of graphs contributes at most $e^{5\delta N\log(1/\delta)+O(\delta N)}$. The maximum of $\delta(-\log\ell+5\log(1/\delta))$ is of order $\ell^{-1/5}$, attained at $\delta\approx\ell^{-1/5}$, which gives the rate $\omega_\lambda=\ell^{-1/5}$. Since this argument does not use the twice-isolated decomposition, $M_0$ does not enter the rate at $\beta=2$. The lower bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:lower bound} is arranged to have the same error.}

  \edblue{For $\beta=2$, we prove \eqref{eq:critical upper} directly from Proposition~\ref{prop:mino2}. Fix an unmarked nearest-neighbor graph $\gamma$ with $K$ components, $m$ isolated pairs and $p_0$ isolated neutral pairs\edgreen{, and write $\{\gamma_{2N}=\gamma\}$ for the set of configurations whose unmarked nearest-neighbor graph is $\gamma$}. Set $d=N-K$, $h=K-m$ and $q=m-p_0$. There are $2d$ tree vertices, so $h\le2d$.

  \edgreen{{\it Energy.}} In each isolated pair retain one endpoint $w_j$ and write the other as $w_j+u_j$. Retain every vertex of the other components. Let $\rho_j$ be one quarter of the distance from $w_j$ to the nearest other retained point, truncated below by $\lambda$, and set $a_j=(|u_j|/4)\vee\lambda$. For either endpoint $z$ of the pair,
  \[
    |u_j|\le4\rho_j,\qquad a_j\le\rho_j,\qquad \rho_j\le3\rr_2(z).
  \]
  Indeed, the second neighbor of $z$ is either retained or paired with a retained point at distance at most its distance to $z$. \edgreen{Since $\rr_1(z)=a_j$, the quadratic error of a neutral pair in Proposition~\ref{prop:mino2} is at most $9C(a_j/\rho_j)^2$ per endpoint, and the correction of a same-sign pair is at least $\log(\rho_j/(3a_j))-C$ per endpoint. Hence} Proposition~\ref{prop:mino2} gives
  \[
    \F_\lambda\ge\F_\lambda^{\nn}
    -C\sum_{j\text{ neutral}}(a_j/\rho_j)^2
    +2\sum_{j\text{ same-sign}}\log(\rho_j/a_j)-C(q+d),
  \]
  where both sums run over isolated pairs. The omitted cycle and tree terms cost at most $Cd$.

  \edgreen{{\it Gaussian decoupling.}} \edgreen{We use Gunson--Panta variables in every component.} Packing gives $\sum_{\text{cycles}}|u_j|^2\le CN$\edgreen{, since the discs $B(z_i,|z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}|/2)$ are disjoint and contained in $[-\sqrt N,2\sqrt N]^2$, and the two endpoints of a cycle are at distance $|u_j|$}. We insert $\exp(-\sum_{\text{cycles}}|u_j|^2/\ell)$ at cost $e^{CN/\ell}$\edgreen{, that is, we use Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian decoupling} with $a=1/\ell$. The divergent integral $\int_{\R^2}e^{2\g_\lambda}$ of a cycle is thereby replaced by $J_\lambda(1/\ell)$, and its part carried by pairs longer than $R$ by $J_\lambda(1/\ell;R)$; both are evaluated in \eqref{eq:critical-B} and \eqref{eq:critical-BR}.}
  \edgreen{By \eqref{eq:critical-correction},} uniformly for $\rho\ge\lambda$, writing $a=(|u|/4)\vee\lambda$, we have
  \[
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}
    \left(e^{C(a/\rho)^2}-1\right)\dd u\le C',\qquad
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)}(a/\rho)^4\dd u\le C'.
  \]
  \edgreen{{\it Integration on a fixed graph.} \edgreen{We integrate first over the variables $u_j$ of the isolated pairs,} with the retained points fixed. Then $\rho_j$ is fixed and $|u_j|\le4\rho_j$. A neutral pair carries the weight
  \[
    e^{2\g_\lambda(u_j)-|u_j|^2/\ell}\,e^{2C(a_j/\rho_j)^2}\,e^{\indic_{|u_j|>R}},
  \]
  where the last factor comes from $e^{D_R}$. Writing $e^{\indic_{|u|>R}}=1+(e-1)\indic_{|u|>R}$ and $e^{2C(a/\rho)^2}=1+(e^{2C(a/\rho)^2}-1)$, the two preceding displays bound its integral by
  \[
    T_R:=\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}+C+(e-1)(\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell;R})+C).
  \]
  A same-sign pair carries $e^{2\g_\lambda(u_j)}(a_j/\rho_j)^4$ up to a constant factor. Its integral is at most $C$: the energy correction removes the logarithmic mass. By \eqref{eq:critical-B} and \eqref{eq:critical-BR},
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-TR}
    \log T_R\le\log \edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}+\frac{C(1+\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell;R}))}{\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}}
    \le\log(2\pi\ell)+C\,\frac{1+\log\ell+\log(1/R)}{\ell}.
  \end{equation}
  This is the only source of the dependence on $R$.} The remaining $h$ cycles contribute $\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}^h$. Their reference points and the $m$ retained endpoints contribute $N^K$. Keeping the packing constraint on the $2d$ tree edges and using the Dirichlet integral with exponent $1/2$ from Lemma~\ref{lemdirichlet} gives the factor $(CN/d)^d$, interpreted as $1$ for $d=0$. Thus
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-fixed-graph}
    \int_{\Lambda^{2N}\cap\{\edgreen{\gamma_{2N}}=\gamma\}}e^{-2\F_\lambda+N-p_0+D_R}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le e^{C(q+d)+N-p_0+CN/\ell}N^K T_R^{p_0}\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}^h\left(\frac{CN}{d}\right)^d.
  \end{equation}
  \edgreen{Compared with the main configuration $p_0=K=N$, which gives $N^N\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}^N$, the bound \eqref{eq:critical-fixed-graph} has $p_0+h=N-d-q$ factors of size about $2\pi\ell$, so it loses a factor of order $\ell^{d+q}$.}

  \edgreen{{\it Counting and optimization.}} We choose the $p_0$ neutral pairs, pair $2(K-p_0)$ remaining labels, and assign a parent to each of the $2d$ tree vertices. Dividing by $p_0!q!h!$ for the unordered cycles bounds the number of graphs by
  \[
    \frac{(N!)^2}{((N-p_0)!)^2}
    \frac{(2N-2p_0)!}{(2d)!\,2^{K-p_0}p_0!q!h!}(2N)^{2d}.
  \]
  Set $\delta=d/N$, $\eta=h/N$ and $\zeta=q/N$. The tilt contributes $N-p_0=N(\delta+\eta+\zeta)$. Combining the count with \eqref{eq:critical-fixed-graph}, Stirling's formula bounds the entropy contribution, divided by $N$, by
  \[
    -(\delta+\zeta)\log\ell
    +3\delta\log(1/\delta)+\eta\log(1/\eta)+\zeta\log(1/\zeta)
    +C(\delta+\eta+\zeta).
  \]
  \edgreen{The term $-(\delta+\zeta)\log\ell$ is the loss $\ell^{-(d+q)}$ noted above. The term $3\delta\log(1/\delta)$ comes from the tree vertices, through the factors $(2N)^{2d}/(2d)!$ and $(CN/d)^d$.} Since $\eta\le2\delta$,
  \[
    \eta\log(1/\eta)\le2\delta\log(1/\delta)+C\delta.
  \]
  Restoring the main term and radial error \edgreen{\eqref{eq:critical-TR}}, and summing over the counts at cost $C\log(N+1)$, gives
  \begin{multline*}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N}} e^{-2\F_\lambda+N-\frac12|I^{\dip}|+D_R}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le2N\log N+N\bigl(\log(2\pi\ell)-1\bigr)\\
    +CN\frac{1+\log\ell+\log(1/R)}{\ell}+C\log(N+1)\\
    +N\sup_{0\le\delta,\zeta\le1}
    \left\{\delta\bigl(-\log\ell+5\log(1/\delta)+C\bigr)
    +\zeta\bigl(-\log\ell+\log(1/\zeta)+C\bigr)\right\}.
  \end{multline*}
  \edgreen{By the inequality of Step 3, applied with coefficients $(C-\log\ell,5)$ and $(C-\log\ell,1)$,
  \[
    \sup_{0\le\delta\le1}\delta\bigl(-\log\ell+5\log(1/\delta)+C\bigr)\le5e^{C/5-1}\ell^{-1/5},
    \qquad
    \sup_{0\le\zeta\le1}\zeta\bigl(-\log\ell+\log(1/\zeta)+C\bigr)\le e^{C-1}\ell^{-1}.
  \]
  The supremum is thus $O(\ell^{-1/5})=O(\omega_\lambda)$. The exponent $1/5$ is the reciprocal of the coefficient $5=3+2$ of $\delta\log(1/\delta)$. Finally, $(1+\log\ell)/\ell\le C\ell^{-1/5}=C\omega_\lambda$, which proves \eqref{eq:critical upper}.} Taking $R=1$ also proves \eqref{ubz} and \eqref{eq:neutral bound} at $\beta=2$, since $N-\frac12|I^{\dip}|\ge (N-\frac12|I^{\dip}|)^2/N$.}
\end{proof}

\section{Free energy lower bound and proof of Theorem \ref{theorem:th1}}\label{sec4}
We now derive a lower bound on the partition function matching \eqref{ubz}. For that, we use a method inspired by previous work on the one-component plasma and on Ginzburg-Landau, starting in  \cite{gl13,SS2d}, which allows us, thanks to the electric formulation of the energy,  to compute the interaction additively in terms of electric potentials defined in disjoint subregions of the space.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:lower bound}
  \edblue{The following estimates hold as $\lambda\to0$, uniformly in $N\ge2$.}
  For $\beta \in (2,+\infty)$, we have
  \begin{multline}\label{lbz}
    \log \Z
    \ge 2N \log N + N(2-\beta) \log \lambda   +
    N(\log \mc{Z}_{\beta}-1)  \\
    + O_\beta\(N(\lambda^{\beta-2}\indic_{\beta<4}+(\lambda^2|\log\lambda|^2)\indic_{\beta=4}+(\lambda^2|\log\lambda|)\indic_{\beta>4})\).
  \end{multline}
  For $\beta=2$, we have
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:lbz2}
    \log \Z\geq 2N\log N+N\log|\log \lambda|+N(\log \mc{Z}_\beta-1)\edblue{-\frac{CN}{|\log\lambda|^{1/5}}}.
  \end{equation}
  \edgreen{At $\beta=2$, the error in \eqref{eq:lbz2} is $CN\omega_\lambda$.}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  {\bf Step 1: bounding the energy from above.}
  \edblue{We restrict $X_N$ to
  \[
    H:=\left\{X_N\in\Lambda^N:\begin{array}{l}
      |x_i-x_j|\ge8\lambda\text{ for }i\ne j,\\
      \dist(x_i,\partial\Lambda)\ge4\lambda\text{ for }1\le i\le N
    \end{array}\right\},
  \]
  and $y_i\in B(x_i,\hal\edblue{\varrho}(x_i))$, where
  \[
    \edblue{\varrho}(x_i):=\min\left\{\hal\min_{j\ne i}|x_j-x_i|,\dist(x_i,\partial\Lambda),1\right\}.
  \]
  For $\lambda$ small, we have $\edblue{\varrho}(x_i)\ge4\lambda$. The balls $B(x_i,\edblue{\varrho}(x_i))$ are disjoint and contained in $\Lambda$, and both smeared charges of the $i$-th pair are supported in $B(x_i,\frac34\edblue{\varrho}(x_i))$.}
  For such configurations let us bound the energy from above. First we recall \eqref{eq:rewrF}.

  We then let, for each $i$,  $u_i$ solve
  $$\left\{ \begin{array}{ll}
    -\Delta u_i= 2\pi (\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} -\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)} )& \text{in} \ B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i))\\
    \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial \nu}= 0 & \text{on} \ \partial B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i))\end{array}\right.,
  $$
  where $\delta_x^{(\lambda)}$ is the uniform measure of mass $1$ supported in $B(x, \lambda)$ and $\nu$ the outer unit normal to the ball.

  \edblue{The Neumann problem is compatible because the source has total mass zero.}

  We then define a global ``electric field" $E$ by pasting together the electric fields defined over these disjoint balls:
  $$E:= \sum_{i=1}^N \indic_{B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i))} \nab u_i.$$
  Thanks to the crucial choice of zero Neumann boundary conditions on the boundary of the disjoint balls, this vector field satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{divE}
    -\div E= 2\pi \left(\sum_{i=1}^N \bigl(\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} -\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)}\bigr)\right) = -\Delta h_\lambda \quad \text{in} \ \R^2,\end{equation}
  where $h_\lambda$ is the electric potential of the configuration  as in \eqref{eq:heta}.
  The trick is then to take advantage of  the $L^2$ projection property onto gradients  to show that the energy can be estimated from above by the $L^2$ norm of $E$:
  indeed $$\int_{\R^2} |E|^2= \int_{\R^2} |\nab h_\lambda|^2 + \int_{\R^2}|E- \nab h_\lambda|^2 + 2 \int_{\R^2} (E-\nab h_\lambda) \cdot \nab h_\lambda$$
  \edblue{Both fields lie in $L^2$. Since $E$ has compact support and neutrality gives $h_\lambda=O(|x|^{-1})$, $\nab h_\lambda=O(|x|^{-2})$, integration by parts with a cutoff at infinity makes the last term vanish by \eqref{divE}.}
  It thus follows that
  \begin{equation}\label{hui}\int_{\R^2} |\nab h_\lambda|^2 \leq  \sum_{i=1}^N \int_{B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i)) } |\nab u_i|^2, \end{equation} and thus we can reduce the computation to a sum over the disjoint balls.
  We next bound the right-hand side.
  First we let $v_i:= u_i - (\g*\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} - \g*\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)})$. It solves
  $$\left\{ \begin{array}{ll}
    -\Delta v_i= 0 & \text{in} \ B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i))\\
    \frac{\partial v_i}{\partial \nu}= \edblue{\( \frac{(x-x_i)}{|x-x_i|^2} - \frac{(x-y_i)}{|x-y_i|^2} \) \cdot \nu}   & \text{on} \ \partial B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i))\end{array}\right.
  $$
  and thus by elliptic regularity estimates we have
  \begin{equation}\label{estimatesv}
    \edblue{\|\nab v_i\|_{L^\infty(B(x_i, \frac34 \edblue{\varrho}(x_i)))}} \le C \frac{|x_i-y_i|}{\edblue{\varrho}(x_i)^2}
    .\end{equation}
  Now, using \eqref{estimatesv},  \eqref{geta0} and \eqref{eq:defgeta} and decomposing $u_i$ as above, we find
  \begin{align*}
    \int_{B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i)) } |\nab u_i|^2 & = 2\pi \int_{B(x_i, \edblue{\varrho}(x_i)) } u_i \(\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} - \delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)}\)
    \\ &= 2\pi \( 2 (\g(\lambda)+\kappa) - \int \g*\delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} \delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)} - \int \g*\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)} \delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} \) + \edblue{2\pi}\int v_i \( \delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} - \delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)}\)
    \\
    &= 4\pi (\g(\lambda)+\kappa)- 4\pi \iint \g(x-y) \delta_{x_i}^{(\lambda)} (x)\delta_{y_i}^{(\lambda)} (y)  + O\Bigr( \frac{|x_i-y_i|^2}{\edblue{\varrho}(x_i)^2}\Bigr)\\
    & = 4\pi (\g(\lambda)+\kappa)- 4\pi \g_\lambda(x_i-y_i)+ O\Bigr( \frac{|x_i-y_i|^2}{\edblue{\varrho}(x_i)^2}\Bigr).
  \end{align*}
  Inserting this into \eqref{hui} and \eqref{eq:rewrF}, we deduce that
  \begin{equation}\label{majof} \F_\lambda(X_N, Y_N) \le  - \sum_{i=1}^N \g_\lambda (x_i-y_i) + O\Bigr(\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{|x_i-y_i|^2}{\edblue{\varrho}(x_i)^2}\Bigr).\end{equation}

  In all cases, since we have built the configurations so that $|x_i-y_i|\le \hal \edblue{\varrho}(x_i)$, the error term is bounded by $O(N)$.

  \edblue{For $\beta=2$, set $t=\ell^{-1/5}$. Restrict the $x_i$'s to boundary distance at least $t$ and mutual distances at least $4t$. Successive placement gives volume at least $N^Ne^{-CNt}$. \edgreen{Restrict each dipole length to $2\lambda\le|x_i-y_i|\le t^2$.} For $\lambda$ small, the disjoint balls $B(x_i,t)$ contain both smeared charges. The preceding Neumann construction gives an energy error at most $CNt^2$. The $N!$ assignments of negative labels are disjoint; hence
  \[
    \Z\ge N!N^Ne^{-CNt-CNt^2}
    \left[2\pi\left(\ell-\tfrac25\log\ell-\log2\right)\right]^N.
  \]
  Using $N!\ge(N/e)^N$ proves \eqref{eq:lbz2}. \edgreen{Here the restriction to $|x_i-y_i|\le t^2$ replaces the large-scale cutoff of the pair mass, as in the discussion before Lemma~\ref{lem:critical radial}: each pair has mass $2\pi\log(t^2/2\lambda)$, which differs from $2\pi\ell$ by $2\pi(2\log(1/t)+\log2)$, while the placement of the $x_i$'s costs $CNt$. The choice $t=\ell^{-1/5}$ makes the total error $O(N\ell^{-1/5})=O(N\omega_\lambda)$, matching \eqref{eq:critical upper}. The choice $t=\ell^{-1}$ would give the smaller error $O(N(1+\log\ell)/\ell)$, but this does not improve Theorem~\ref{theorem:th1}.} We now assume $\beta>2$.}
  \smallskip

  \noindent {\bf Step 2: bounding the free energy.}
  \edblue{For $r\ge4\lambda$, define
  \[
    J_\beta(r):=\int_0^{r/2}2\pi s\exp\left(\beta\g_\lambda(s)-A_\beta\frac{s^2}{r^2}\right)\dd s,
  \]
  with $A_\beta>0$ fixed large enough in view of \eqref{majof}. The $N!$ assignments of negative labels are disjoint; hence
  \begin{equation}\label{minz3}
    \Z\ge N!\int_H\prod_{i=1}^N J_\beta(\edblue{\varrho}(x_i))\dd X_N.
  \end{equation}
  By scaling and restricting the radial integral to $[0,2\lambda]$, we have
  \[
    c_\beta\le\frac{J_\beta(r)}{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}}\le1.
  \]
  Moreover, using $1-e^{-t}\le t$ for $t\ge0$, we obtain
  \begin{align*}
    0\le\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}-J_\beta(r)
    &\le C_\beta\left(r^{2-\beta}+\frac1{r^2}\int_0^{r/2}\frac{s^3}{(s\vee\lambda)^\beta}\dd s\right)\\
    &\le C_\beta\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}\varphi_\beta(r),
  \end{align*}
  where $\varphi_\beta$ is defined in \eqref{defvarphi}. The preceding two bounds imply
  \begin{equation}\label{align57}
    \log\frac{J_\beta(r)}{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}}\ge-C_\beta\varphi_\beta(r).
  \end{equation}

  A boundary margin $b$ removes area at most $4\sqrt N b$, and exclusion discs of radius $r$ remove at most $(N-1)\pi r^2$. With $b=4\lambda$ and $r=8\lambda$, successive placement leaves area at least $N-C\sqrt N\lambda-CN\lambda^2\ge N/2$. Using $1-x\ge e^{-2x}$ for $0\le x\le1/2$ gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:volume-lower}
    |H|\ge N^N\exp(-C\sqrt N\lambda-CN\lambda^2).
  \end{equation}
  Let $\mathbb Q$ be normalized Lebesgue measure on $H$. By Jensen's inequality and symmetry, \eqref{minz3} and \eqref{align57} give
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:Jensen}
    \log\Z\ge\log N!+\log|H|+N\log(\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta})
    -C_\beta N\Esp_{\mathbb Q}[\varphi_\beta(\edblue{\varrho}(x_1))].
  \end{equation}}

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  {\bf Step 3: conclusion.}
  \edblue{Given all other points, each $x_i$ is uniform on a set of area at least $N/2$. Its conditional density is therefore at most $2/N$. The union bound for the other particles and the area of the boundary strip give
  \[
    \mathbb Q(\edblue{\varrho}(x_1)\le r)\le C\left(r^2+\frac r{\sqrt N}\right),\qquad 4\lambda\le r<1.
  \]
  \edgreen{Set}
  \[
    \varepsilon_\lambda:=\begin{cases}
      \lambda^{\beta-2},&2<\beta<4,\\
      \lambda^2\ell^2,&\beta=4,\\
      \lambda^2\ell,&\beta>4,
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    \eta_\lambda:=\begin{cases}
      \lambda^{\beta-2},&2<\beta<3,\\
      \lambda\ell,&\beta=3,\\
      \lambda,&\beta>3.
    \end{cases}
  \]
  Since $\varphi_\beta$ is decreasing, integration of the preceding probability bound yields
  \begin{align*}
    \Esp_{\mathbb Q}[\varphi_\beta(\edblue{\varrho}(x_1))]
    &\le\varphi_\beta(1)+C\int_{4\lambda}^1(-\varphi_\beta'(r))\left(r^2+\frac r{\sqrt N}\right)\dd r\\
    &\le C_\beta\left(\varepsilon_\lambda+\frac{\eta_\lambda}{\sqrt N}\right).
  \end{align*}
  Since $\lambda^2\le\varepsilon_\lambda$ and $\lambda\le\eta_\lambda$, \eqref{eq:volume-lower} and \eqref{eq:Jensen} imply
  \begin{equation*}
    \log\Z\ge\log N!+N\log N+N\log(\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta})
    -C_\beta N\varepsilon_\lambda-C_\beta\sqrt N\eta_\lambda.
  \end{equation*}
  Finally, we have
  \[
    C_\beta\sqrt N\eta_\lambda\le N\varepsilon_\lambda+C_\beta'\frac{\eta_\lambda^2}{\varepsilon_\lambda},\qquad
    \frac{\eta_\lambda^2}{\varepsilon_\lambda}=o_\lambda(1).
  \]
  Using $\log N!\ge N\log N-N+1$ proves \eqref{lbz} for $\lambda$ small enough.}
\end{proof}

\medskip

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{theorem:th1}]
  \edblue{We take $\lambda_\beta<e^{-1}$ below the thresholds of Lemma~\ref{lemma:part nn} and Propositions~\ref{prop:upper bound} and~\ref{prop:lower bound}, including their critical bounds. These estimates give $0<\Z<\infty$. The lower-bound error is $O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)$\edgreen{. Indeed, for $\lambda\le e^{-1}$ we have $\lambda\le\lambda|\log\lambda|\le1$, hence
  \[
    \lambda^{\beta-2}\le\lambda^{\frac{2(\beta-2)}{84-\beta}}\ (2<\beta<4),\qquad
    \lambda^2|\log\lambda|^2\le(\lambda|\log\lambda|)^{\frac1{21}}\ (\beta=4),\qquad
    \lambda^2|\log\lambda|\le\lambda\le\lambda^{\frac1{20}}\ (\beta>4),
  \]
  and the error in \eqref{eq:lbz2} is $CN\omega_\lambda$}, so combining the two bounds proves \eqref{eq:devlogz}.}

  Let $\omega_\lambda$ be as in \eqref{eq:defgammal}. Define
  \begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\F}_\lambda:=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\edblue{2N}} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)})\indic_{\phi_1\circ \phi_1(i)=i,d_id_{\phi_1(i)}=-1}
  \end{equation*}
  and
  \begin{equation*}
    \K_{N,\beta}^\lambda:=\int_{\edblue{\Lambda^{2N}}} e^{-\beta \tilde{\F}_\lambda(X_N,Y_N)}\dd X_N\dd Y_N.
  \end{equation*}
  One has
  \begin{equation*}
    \dE_{\P}[\exp(\beta( \F_\lambda-\tilde{\F}_\lambda))]=\frac{\K_{N,\beta}^\lambda}{\Z}.
  \end{equation*}
  By Lemma \ref{lemma:part nn}, there holds
  \begin{multline*}
    \log \K_{N,\beta}^\lambda=2N\log N+N((2-\beta)(\log \lambda )\indic_{\beta>2}+(\log|\log\lambda|)\indic_{\beta=2})\\
    +N(\log\mc{Z}_\beta-1)+O_\beta(N\bar\omega_\lambda)\edblue{+O_\beta(\log(N+1))},
  \end{multline*}
  where
  \begin{equation*}
    \bar\omega_\lambda=\begin{cases}
      |\log \lambda|^{-\nicefrac{1}{2}} & \text{if $\beta=2$}\\
      \edblue{\lambda^{\min\{(\beta-2)/2,1\}}} & \text{if $\beta>2$}.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  Since $\bar\omega_\lambda\leq \omega_\lambda$\edgreen{, which holds because $|\log\lambda|^{-1/2}\le|\log\lambda|^{-1/5}$, $\frac{\beta-2}2\ge\frac{2(\beta-2)}{84-\beta}$ for $2<\beta<4$, and $\lambda\le\omega_\lambda$ for $\beta\ge4$}, we get by \eqref{eq:devlogz} that
  \begin{equation*}
    \log \frac{\K_{N,\beta}^\lambda}{\Z}=NO_\beta(\omega_\lambda)\edblue{+O_\beta(\log(N+1))},
  \end{equation*}
  which concludes the proof of \eqref{eq:momexp}.

  \edblue{The events in item (3) are measurable, since they are finite unions of sets defined by distance comparisons. For each such event $A$,
  \[
    \P(A)=\frac1{\Z}\int_{\Lambda^{2N}\cap A}e^{-\beta\F_\lambda}\dd Z_{2N}.
  \]
  Subtracting the lower bound from \eqref{eq:neutral bound} proves \eqref{eq:b1}.

  For $\beta>2$ and $s\ge2$, the identity $\g_1(s)=-\log s$ gives
  \[
    \mu_\beta([s,\infty))=C_\mu s^{2-\beta},
    \qquad C_\mu=\frac{2\pi}{(\beta-2)\mc Z_\beta}.
  \]
  \edgreen{Set} $\alpha=(\lambda/R)^{\beta-2}$. Taking the threshold in \eqref{eq:b2} at least $\max\{C_\beta C_\mu,C_\mu^2,2\}$ ensures the hypothesis of \eqref{eq:u bound} and
  \[
    \log\frac{x_0}{C_\mu\alpha}\ge\frac12\log\frac{x_0}{\alpha}.
  \]
  Subtracting the lower bound proves \eqref{eq:b2}\edgreen{, since the errors in \eqref{eq:u bound} and \eqref{lbz} are both $O_\beta(N\omega_\lambda)$}. For $\beta=2$, subtracting \eqref{eq:lbz2} from \eqref{eq:critical upper} and applying Markov's inequality gives \eqref{eq:critical lengths}, since $N-\frac12|I^{\dip}|\ge0$.}
\end{proof}

\section{Energetic control on linear statistics}\label{sec5}
In this section, we \edblue{use} our ball-growth method introduced in Section \ref{sec2} to derive an energetic control on the fluctuations of linear statistics, similar to \cite[Prop. 2.5]{LS2} for the one-component plasma. \edblue{We prove Theorem~\ref{prop:linear}, giving a fluctuation bound at scale $\sqrt N\,\omega_\lambda^{1/4}$ for Lipschitz test-functions.} Let us emphasize that linear statistics are in fact expected to fluctuate much less, specifically at a rate $o_N(\sqrt{N})$ for small but fixed $\lambda$. Proving such a rigidity statement would require more involved techniques.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{prop:linear}]
  {\bf Step 1: the electric energy bounds the fluctuations.} As in the one-component case \cite{ss1d,LS2} or in \cite{LSZ}, the fluctuations are well bounded by the electric energy $
  \int |\nab h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2$, where $h_{\vec{\alpha}}$ is as in  \eqref{defhalpha}, \edblue{for every $\vec\alpha\in(0,\infty)^{2N}$.} We recall the elementary argument.

  Let $\xi$ be a compactly supported Lipschitz test-function from $\R^2 $ to $\R$.
  \edblue{Let $\tilde\xi$ be $\xi-\xi(0)$ multiplied by a cutoff equal to $1$ on $\Lambda$, supported in $[-\sqrt N,2\sqrt N]^2$ and with gradient bounded by $C/\sqrt N$. Then $\Fluct_N(\tilde\xi)=\Fluct_N(\xi)$ and
  \[
    \|\nab\tilde\xi\|_{L^\infty}+N^{-1/2}\|\nab\tilde\xi\|_{L^2(\R^2)}\le C\|\nab\xi\|_{L^\infty}.
  \]}
  \edblue{Neutrality gives $\nab h_{\vec\alpha}\in L^2$. We apply Green's formula to mollifications of $\tilde\xi$ and pass to the limit in $W^{1,2}$. The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives}
  \begin{equation*}
    \left|\int_{\edblue{\R^2}}\edblue{\tilde\xi} \, \dd\( 2\pi \sum_{i=1}^{2N} d_i \delta_{z_i}^{(\alpha_i)}\)\right|=
    \left|\int_{\R^2} \edblue{\tilde\xi} \Delta h_{\vec{\alpha}}\right|\le \edblue{\|\nab\tilde\xi\|_{L^2(\R^2)}}\( \int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2\)^\hal.\end{equation*}
  On the other hand, by definition of the smeared charges, we may write
  \begin{equation*}
    \left|\int_{\edblue{\R^2}} \edblue{\tilde\xi} \, \dd\(  2\pi \sum_{i=1}^{2N} d_i (\delta_{z_i}- \delta_{z_i}^{(\alpha_i)})\)
    \right|\le C \edblue{\|\nab\tilde\xi\|_{L^\infty}} \sum_{i=1}^{2N} \alpha_i.\end{equation*}
  Combining the two relations, we  deduce that
  \begin{equation*}
    \left|\Fluct_N(\xi)\right|\le C \|\nab \xi\|_{L^\infty} \(\sqrt N\( \int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2\)^\hal+ \sum_{i=1}^{2N} \alpha_i\),
  \end{equation*}
  hence
  \begin{equation*}
    \left|\Fluct_N(\xi)\right|^2 \le C \|\nab \xi\|_{L^\infty}^2 \(N \int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2+ \(\sum_{i=1}^{2N} \alpha_i\)^2\).\end{equation*}
  If $\xi(x)= \xi_0( x/\sqrt{N})$, then $\|\nab \xi\|_{L^\infty} \le  \frac{\|\nab \xi_0\|_{L^\infty}}{\sqrt N}$. It follows that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq1}
    \left|\Fluct_N(\xi)\right|^2 \le C \|\nab \xi_0\|_{L^\infty}^2 \(\int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2+\frac{1}{N}\(\sum_{i=1}^{2N} \alpha_i\)^2\).\end{equation}
  \smallskip

  \noindent
  {\bf Step 2: upper bound for the electric energy by the ball-growth method.}
  The proof consists in repeating the steps of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:mino2}. Recall the radii defined in \eqref{deftau},
  \begin{equation*}
    \tau_i= \begin{cases}
      \rr_2(z_{i})\wedge  \rr_2(z_{\phi_1(i) })  & \text{if $i\in \mathcal C_k$}\\
      \rr_2(z_i) & \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  We let

  \begin{equation}\label{eq:def alphai}
    \alpha_i:=  (\tau_i \wedge \gamma) \vee \rr_1(z_i)\end{equation}
  for a parameter $\edblue{2\lambda\le\gamma\le1}$ to be chosen later.

  \edblue{On a $2$-cycle, \eqref{rrj} and $\tau_i\ge\rr_1(z_i)$ give $\tau_i\ge\rr_2(z_i)/2$. Hence
  \[
    \alpha_i\ge\frac12\bigl((\rr_2(z_i)\wedge\gamma)\vee\rr_1(z_i)\bigr).
  \]
  We also have $\lambda\le\rr_1(z_i)\le\alpha_i\le\tau_i$ for every $i$, while $\alpha_i=\alpha_{\phi_1(i)}$ on a $2$-cycle. Thus $|z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}|/\alpha_i\le4$, as required for the kernel estimate.}

  We then bound $\int |\nabla h_{\lambda}|^2$ from below as in the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:mino2}  and find
  \begin{align*}
    & \int_{\R^2} |\nab h_{\lambda}|^2
    \ge  \int_{\R^2} |\nab h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2   \\
    &
    + 2\pi\sum_{k=1}^K \Bigg( \sum_{i\in \mathcal C_k} \edblue{\Bigl[}d_i d_{\phi_1(i)} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} ) -d_i \( d_i+ d_{\phi_1(i)} \)(\g(\edblue{\alpha_i})+\kappa) \\
    & + O\Bigr( \Bigr( \frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{(\rr_2(z_i)\wedge \gamma) \vee \rr_1(z_i) }\Bigr)^2\Bigr)\edblue{\Bigr]}
    -\sum_{i\in \mathcal{I}_k \backslash \mathcal C_k} \edblue{\bigl(}\g(\edblue{\alpha_i})+\kappa\edblue{\bigr)} \Bigg)+4N\pi  \g_\lambda(0).
  \end{align*}
  \edblue{For $\beta=2$, we use this inequality directly. Let $S=1+\log\ell+\log(1/\gamma)$. We use the retained coordinates of the proof of \eqref{eq:critical upper}, with $a=(|u|/4)\vee\lambda$. The neutral pair error is bounded by $C((a/\rho)^2+\min\{a/\gamma,1\}^2)$, and the same-sign correction is at least $2\log(((\rho\wedge\gamma)\vee a)/a)-C$.

  \edgreen{Recall $J_\lambda(1/\ell)$ and $J_\lambda(1/\ell;R)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:critical radial}.} Splitting at $2\lambda$ and $4\gamma$ gives, uniformly for $\rho\ge\lambda$,
  \begin{align*}
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)-|u|^2/\ell}
    e^{C((a/\rho)^2+\min\{a/\gamma,1\}^2)}\dd u&\le \edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}+C'S,\\
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{2\g_\lambda(u)-|u|^2/\ell}
    \left(\frac a{(\rho\wedge\gamma)\vee a}\right)^4\dd u&\le C'S.
  \end{align*}
  Indeed, the cores and the quadratic correction contribute $O(1)$. Beyond $4\gamma$, the Gaussian radial tail is $O(1+\log\ell+\log(1/\gamma))$. \edgreen{More precisely, by \eqref{eq:critical-correction} applied with $\rho$ and with $\gamma$, the factor $e^{C(a/\rho)^2}$ and the part $|u|<4\gamma$ of the factor $e^{C\min\{a/\gamma,1\}^2}$ add $O(1)$ to $J_\lambda(1/\ell)$. On $|u|\ge4\gamma$, this factor is at most $e^C$, and the integral of $e^{2\g_\lambda(u)-|u|^2/\ell}$ there is at most $J_\lambda(1/\ell;(4\gamma)\wedge1)=O(S)$ by \eqref{eq:critical-BR}, since $2\lambda\le\gamma\le1$. For the second bound, we use
  \[
    \Bigl(\frac a{(\rho\wedge\gamma)\vee a}\Bigr)^4\le\Bigl(\frac a\rho\Bigr)^4+\min\Bigl\{\frac a\gamma,1\Bigr\}^4,
  \]
  bound the first term by \eqref{eq:critical-correction}, and the second term as above, by \eqref{eq:critical-correction} with $\rho=\gamma$ on $|u|<4\gamma$ and by $J_\lambda(1/\ell;(4\gamma)\wedge1)$ on $|u|\ge4\gamma$. Thus the cutoff $\gamma$ acts like the length threshold $R=4\gamma$ in the proof of \eqref{eq:critical upper}, and $S/\ell$ plays the role of the relative error in \eqref{eq:critical-TR}.}

  On an unmarked graph cell $\Lambda^{2N}\cap\{\edgreen{\gamma_{2N}}=\gamma'\}$, let $K,m,p_0$ count components, isolated pairs and neutral isolated pairs. \edgreen{Set} $d=N-K$, $h=K-m$ and $q=m-p_0$, so $h\le2d$. For $0<c\le1/(2\pi)$, the electric term is absorbed in $-2\F_\lambda$. The preceding pair bounds, the Gaussian insertion and the remaining cycle and tree integrals give
  \begin{equation*}
    \int_{\Lambda^{2N}\cap\{\edgreen{\gamma_{2N}}=\gamma'\}}e^{-2\F_\lambda+c\int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec\alpha}|^2}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le e^{C(q+d)+CN/\ell}N^K(\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}+CS)^{p_0}(CS)^q\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}^h
    \left(\frac{CN}{d}\right)^d,
  \end{equation*}
  with the last factor equal to $1$ when $d=0$.
  We use the graph count from the proof of \eqref{eq:critical upper}. Since
  $\log(\edgreen{J_\lambda(1/\ell)}+CS)\le\log(2\pi\ell)+CS/\ell$, the same Stirling computation, with $\delta=d/N$ and $\zeta=q/N$, gives
  \begin{multline*}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N}}e^{-2\F_\lambda+c\int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec\alpha}|^2}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le2N\log N+N\bigl(\log(2\pi\ell)-1\bigr)
    +\frac{CNS}{\ell}+C\log(N+1)\\
    +N\sup_{0\le\delta,\zeta\le1}\left\{
    \delta\left[-\log\ell+5\log(1/\delta)+C\right]
    +\zeta\left[-\log\ell+\log S+\log(1/\zeta)+C\right]\right\}.
  \end{multline*}
  The two suprema are bounded by $C\ell^{-1/5}$ and $CS/\ell$\edgreen{, by the inequality of Step 3 of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:upper bound}. Here $\ell^{-1/5}=\omega_\lambda$, and $S/\ell\le\varphi_2(\gamma)+C\omega_\lambda$}. Dividing by the partition function and using \eqref{eq:lbz2} yields
  \begin{multline}\label{eq:critical electric}
    \log\Esp_{\P}\left[e^{c\int_{\R^2}|\nabla h_{\vec\alpha}|^2}\right]
    \le CN\left(\ell^{-1/5}+\frac S\ell\right)+C\log(N+1)\\
    \le CN\bigl(\omega_\lambda+\varphi_2(\gamma)\bigr)+C\log(N+1).
  \end{multline}
  For the remainder of Steps 2--4, assume $\beta>2$.}

  \edblue{Since $\rr_1(z_i)\le\rr_2(z_i)$, we have
  \[
    \left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{(\rr_2(z_i)\wedge\gamma)\vee\rr_1(z_i)}\right)^2
    \le\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\right)^2
    +\left(\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}\gamma\right)^2\wedge1\right).
  \]
  Arguing as in the proof of Corollary~\ref{coromino}, we obtain
  \begin{multline*}
    \F_\lambda(X_N,Y_N)\ge\frac1{4\pi}\int_{\R^2}|\nab h_{\vec\alpha}|^2
    -\hal\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)})\\
    +\sum_{\substack{i\in I^\pair\setminus I^\dip\\\phi_2(i),\phi_2(\phi_1(i))\in I^\pair}}
    \left(\log\frac{\alpha_i}{\rr_1(z_i)}-C\right)\\
    -C\sum_{\substack{i\in I^\dip\\\phi_2(i),\phi_2(\phi_1(i))\in I^\pair}}
    \left[\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i)}\right)^2
    +\left(\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}\gamma\right)^2\wedge1\right)\right]-C(N-K).
  \end{multline*}
  By Remark~\ref{remark:maximal5}, at most $12|I^\pair\setminus I^\dip|$ indices in the neutral sum have $\phi_2(i)\notin I^\dip$. Their summands are at most $2$. Since $|I^\pair\setminus I^\dip|\le2N-|I^\dip|$ and $|I^\dip|/2\le K$, we may write}
  \begin{equation*}
    \frac1{4\pi}  \int_{\R^2} |\nabla h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2\le A_1+C (A_2+A_3),
  \end{equation*}
  with
  \begin{align*}
    A_1&:= \F_\lambda(Z_{2N})+ \hal \sum_{ i=1}^{2N} \g_\lambda(z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)} )-  \sum_{\substack{i\in I^{\pair}\backslash I^{\dip}\\ \phi_2(i) \in I^\pair, \phi_2(\phi_1(i)) \in I^\pair}}\( \log
    \frac{\edblue{\alpha_i}}{\rr_1(z_i)}-C\),
    \\
    A_2&:=\sum_{i\in I^{\dip},\phi_2(i)\in I^{\dip},\phi_2(\phi_1(i)) \in I^\pair } \Bigr(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\rr_2(z_i) } \Bigr)^2 + \edblue{\left(N-\frac12|I^\dip|\right)},\\
    A_3&:=\sum_{i\in I^{\dip},\phi_2(i)\in I^{\dip} ,\phi_2(\phi_1(i)) \in I^\pair} \edblue{\Bigl(}\Bigr(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{{\gamma} } \Bigr)^2 \wedge 1\edblue{\Bigr)}.
  \end{align*}
  {\bf Step 3: bounding an exponential moment of $A_1$ and $A_2$.}
  \edblue{In the Gibbs integrand $e^{\beta(A_1-\F_\lambda)}$, the $\F_\lambda$ term of $A_1$ cancels exactly. We repeat the proof of \eqref{eq:alt}, keeping $\gamma$ in the same-sign term. On a marked graph, let $|I^\pair|=2m$ and $q=p-p_0$. For $m\ge2$, we use the representatives of Proposition~\ref{prop:isolated}, with $\rho$ as in its proof and $a=(|u|/4)\vee\lambda$. The normalized same-sign radial factor is bounded by
  \begin{equation*}
    \frac{C_\beta}{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}}
    \int_{|u|\le4\rho}e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u)}
    \left(\frac{a}{(\rho\wedge\gamma)\vee a}\right)^{2\beta}\dd u
    \le C_\beta\left[\left(\frac\lambda\rho\right)^{\beta-2}
    +\left(\frac\lambda\gamma\right)^{\beta-2}\right].
  \end{equation*}
  Indeed, the factor in the integrand is at most $(a/\rho)^{2\beta}+\min\{a/\gamma,1\}^{2\beta}$. The first term is bounded by \eqref{align2}, and we integrate the second over $\R^2$.

  We expand the added constant over $s$ of the $q$ marked same-sign pairs. The entropy comparison preceding \eqref{eq:F(1)} bounds the entropy factor by $(m+1)\binom mq e^{C_0q}$. For $0\le s\le q$, we use
  \[
    \binom mq\binom qs=\binom m{q-s}\binom{m-q+s}s,
    \qquad \binom{m-q+s}s\le\binom ms.
  \]
  We use \eqref{eq:representative-bound} for the remaining integrals, assigning the $s$ pairs the factors $1+C_\beta\varphi_\beta(\rho)\ge1$. Summing the constant terms costs at most
  \[
    \left(1+C_\beta e^{C_0}(\lambda/\gamma)^{\beta-2}\right)^m
    \le\exp\bigl(C_\beta N\varphi_\beta(\gamma)\bigr).
  \]
  For $p<N/2$, the same crude bound applies, including $m\le1$. After reducing $\lambda_\beta$, the graph sum and Proposition~\ref{prop:lower bound} give
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A1}
    \log\Esp_{\P}[e^{\beta A_1}]
    \le C_\beta N\bigl(\omega_\lambda+\varphi_\beta(\gamma)\bigr)+C_\beta\log(N+1).
  \end{equation}}
  \edblue{To bound $A_2$, we repeat Steps 2 and 3 of Proposition~\ref{prop:upper bound} with a larger coefficient in the neutral quadratic term. On each marked graph,
  \[
    N-\frac12|I^\dip|\le N-p_0\le(M_0+3)(N-K)+(p-p_0).
  \]
  We absorb the first term into $C_\beta(N-K)$ and the second into the arbitrary $C_0(p-p_0)$ allowance in \eqref{eq:representative-bound}. Dividing by the partition function gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:A2}
    \log\Esp_{\P}[e^{A_2}]\le C_\beta N\omega_\lambda+C_\beta\log(N+1).
  \end{equation}}
  {\bf Step 4: bounding an exponential moment of $A_3$.} \edblue{Fix $t>0$ and assume $2\lambda\le\gamma\le1$. We use the marked graph decomposition of Proposition~\ref{prop:upper bound}:
  \begin{multline*}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N}} e^{tA_3-\beta\F_\lambda}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le\max_{K,p,p_0}\Bigg(\log|D_{2N,K,p,p_0}|\\
    +\max_{\gamma'\in D_{2N,K,p,p_0}}
    \log\int_{\Omega_{\gamma'}}e^{tA_3-\beta\F_\lambda}\dd Z_{2N}\Bigg)+C\log(N+1).
  \end{multline*}
  Fix $\gamma'\in D_{2N,K,p,p_0}$, and let $I=I^{\pair}$, $|I|=2m$, with restrictions ${\gamma'}^1$ and ${\gamma'}^2$ as in \eqref{gred3}. On this domain,
  \[
    A_3\le\sum_{i\in I}\left(\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\gamma}\right)^2\wedge1\right),
  \]
  where the distances on the right are computed within $I$. Hence
  \begin{multline*}
    \log\int_{\Omega_{\gamma'}}e^{tA_3-\beta\F_\lambda}\dd Z_{2N}\\
    \le\log\int_{\Lambda^{2m}\cap\{\gamma_{2m}={\gamma'}^1\}}
    \exp\left(t\sum_{i\in I}\left(\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\gamma}\right)^2\wedge1\right)-\beta F^{(1)}\right)\prod_{i\in I}\dd z_i\\
    +\log\int_{\Lambda^{2N-2m}\cap\{\gamma_{2N-2m}={\gamma'}^2\}}e^{-\beta F^{(2)}}\prod_{i\in I^c}\dd z_i+C_\beta(N-K).
  \end{multline*}
  For a pair in \edgreen{$I\setminus I'$}, the extra radial contribution is controlled by
  \[
    \frac{1}{\mc Z_\beta\lambda^{2-\beta}}
    \int_{\R^2}e^{\beta\g_\lambda(u)}
    \left(\left(\frac{(|u|/4)\vee\lambda}{\gamma}\right)^2\wedge1\right)\dd u
    \le C_\beta\varphi_\beta(\gamma).
  \]
  This follows by splitting at $2\lambda$ and $4\gamma$. For $0\le s\le1$,
  \[
    e^{2ts}\le1+(e^{2t}-1)s.
  \]
  The unperturbed quadratic factor is bounded on $|u|\le4\rho$. Hence the tilt adds at most $C_{\beta,t}\varphi_\beta(\gamma)$ to the normalized \edgreen{radial factor}, bounded by
  \[
    (1+C_{\beta,t}\varphi_\beta(\gamma))(1+C_\beta\varphi_\beta(\rho)).
  \]
  For each marked same-sign pair, the tilt is at most $e^{2t}$, absorbed by increasing $C_0$. Thus \eqref{eq:representative-bound} and the entropy comparison above \eqref{eq:F(1)} give, for $p\ge N/2$,
  \begin{multline*}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2m}\cap\{\gamma_{2m}={\gamma'}^1\}}
    \exp\left(t\sum_{i\in I}\left(\left(\frac{\rr_1(z_i)}{\gamma}\right)^2\wedge1\right)-\beta F^{(1)}\right)\prod_{i\in I}\dd z_i\\
    +p\log p-p_0\log p_0-(p-p_0)\log(p-p_0)+C_0(p-p_0)\\
    \le m\bigl(\log N+(2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta\bigr)
    +O_{\beta,t}\bigl(N(\varphi_\beta(\gamma)+\omega_\lambda)\bigr)
    -c_{\beta,t}N\left(\frac{p-p_0}{N}\right)^2+C_{\beta,t}\log(N+1).
  \end{multline*}
  For $p<N/2$, the tilt is at most $e^{2tm}$, so the crude bound suffices as in Step 3 of the upper-bound proof. Taking $0<\lambda<\lambda_{\beta,t}$ for a threshold depending only on $\beta,t$, combining with \eqref{eq:F(2)} and summing over the marked graphs yields
  \begin{multline*}
    \log\int_{\Lambda^{2N}} e^{tA_3-\beta\F_\lambda}\dd Z_{2N}
    \le2N\log N+N\bigl((2-\beta)\log\lambda+\log\mc Z_\beta-1\bigr)\\
    +O_{\beta,t}\bigl(N(\varphi_\beta(\gamma)+\omega_\lambda)\bigr)+C_{\beta,t}\log(N+1).
  \end{multline*}
  Subtracting the lower bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:lower bound} gives
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:finalA3}
    \log\dE_{\P}[e^{tA_3}]
    \le C_{\beta,t}N\bigl(\varphi_\beta(\gamma)+\omega_\lambda\bigr)+C_{\beta,t}\log(N+1),
  \end{equation}
  with $\varphi_\beta$ as in \eqref{defvarphi}.}

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  {\bf Step 5: control on the radii.} \edblue{Set
  \[
    R_0=\begin{cases}
      \omega_\lambda^{1/4}&\text{if $\beta=2$},\\
      \lambda\omega_\lambda^{-1/(2(\beta-2))}\edgreen{\vee2\lambda}&\text{if $\beta>2$},
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    \mc A_M=\{N-\frac12|I^{\dip}|+D_{R_0}\le MN\sqrt{\omega_\lambda}\}.
  \]
  For $\lambda$ small, $2\lambda\le R_0\le\omega_\lambda^{1/4}$\edgreen{; for $\beta>2$, this only requires $2\lambda\le\omega_\lambda^{1/4}$, and $\alpha=(\lambda/R_0)^{\beta-2}\le\omega_\lambda^{1/2}$}. At $\beta=2$, we use \eqref{eq:b1} and \eqref{eq:critical lengths}, since
  \[
    \edgreen{\frac{\log(1/R_0)}{|\log\lambda|}=\frac{\log|\log\lambda|}{20\,|\log\lambda|}=o(\omega_\lambda).}
  \]
  For $\beta>2$, we use \eqref{eq:b1} and \eqref{eq:b2}. A union bound and summation over the counts give, for $M$ large,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:PAM}
    \P(\mc A_M^c)\le C_\beta(N+1)^{C_\beta}e^{-c_\beta MN\omega_\lambda}.
  \end{equation}
  On $\mc A_M$, at most $2MN\sqrt{\omega_\lambda}$ particles lie outside isolated dipoles of length at most $R_0$. The balls $B(z_i,|z_i-z_{\phi_1(i)}|/2)$ are disjoint in $[-\sqrt N,2\sqrt N]^2$, so $\sum_i\rr_1(z_i)^2\le CN+2N\lambda^2\le C'N$. Thus
  \[
    \sum_{i=1}^{2N}\rr_1(z_i)
    \le2NR_0+\left(2MN\sqrt{\omega_\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\rr_1(z_i)^2\right)^{1/2}
    \le C(1+\sqrt M)N\omega_\lambda^{1/4}.
  \]
  It follows that
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:sumrr1}
    \left(\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\rr_1(z_i)\right)^2\le C(1+M)N^2\sqrt{\omega_\lambda}.
  \end{equation}}

  \smallskip
  \noindent
  {\bf Step 6: conclusion.} \edblue{For $\beta>2$, fix $t=1$ in \eqref{eq:finalA3} and choose $c_\beta>0$ so that $12\pi c_\beta\le\beta$ and $12\pi c_\beta C\le1$, with $C$ from Step 2. H\"older's inequality with three factors and
  \[
    \Esp_{\P}[e^{sX}]\le\bigl(\Esp_{\P}[e^{rX}]\bigr)^{s/r},\qquad 0\le s\le r,\quad r>0,
  \]
  give the following bound from \eqref{eq:A1}, \eqref{eq:A2} and \eqref{eq:finalA3}. For $\beta=2$, it follows from \eqref{eq:critical electric}.}
  \begin{equation*}
    \log\Esp_{\P}\left[\exp\(c_\beta \int_{\R^2} |\nabla h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2 \)\right]\leq C_\beta N(\varphi_\beta(\gamma)+\omega_\lambda)\edblue{+C_\beta\log(N+1)}.
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore, there exists an event $\mc{B}_M$ such that for all $M$ large enough,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq2}
    \int_{\R^2} |\nabla h_{\vec{\alpha}}|^2\leq C_\beta M N (\varphi_\beta(\gamma)+\omega_\lambda)\quad\text{on }\mc B_M,
  \end{equation}
  \begin{equation*}
    \P(\mc{B}_M^c)\leq C_\beta \edblue{(N+1)^{C_\beta}}e^{-c_\beta M N\omega_\lambda}.
  \end{equation*}
  Combining the last displays and \eqref{eq:sumrr1} and in view of the definition of $\alpha_i$, we deduce that \edblue{for $M$ large enough,} we have
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:f}
    |\Fluct_N(\xi)|^2\leq \edblue{C_\beta M}\Vert \nabla \xi_0 \Vert_{L^{\infty}}\edblue{^2}N({\gamma}^2+\omega_\lambda^{\ed{\nicefrac{1}{2}}}+\varphi_\beta(\gamma))\quad \text{on the event $\mc{A}_M\cap \mc{B}_M$.}
  \end{equation}
  Optimizing over $\gamma$, we may then choose $\gamma$ as follows:
  \begin{equation*}
    \gamma=\begin{cases}
      |\log\lambda|^{-1/2} &\text{if $\beta=2$}\\
      \lambda^{\frac{(\beta-2)}{\beta}}& \text{if $\beta\in (2,4)$}\\
      \lambda^{1/2}|\log\lambda|^{1/4}& \text{if $\beta=4$}\\
      \lambda^{1/2}& \text{if $\beta\in (4,\infty)$}.
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  \edblue{We reduce $\lambda_\beta$ so that all previous estimates and $2\lambda\le\gamma,R_0\le1$ hold.} Noting that for $\gamma$ defined as above,
  $\varphi_\beta(\gamma)+\gamma^2=O_\beta(\omega_\lambda)$, \edblue{we combine \eqref{eq:f}, \eqref{eq:PAM} and the bound on $\P(\mc B_M^c)$. The elementary bound
  \[
    \min\{1,C_\beta(N+1)^{C_\beta}e^{-c_\beta MN\omega_\lambda}\}
    \le C_\beta'e^{-c_\beta'M\sqrt N\,\omega_\lambda}
  \]
  absorbs the polynomial prefactor. Replacing the event parameter $M$ by $M/C_\beta$ concludes the proof.}
\end{proof}
